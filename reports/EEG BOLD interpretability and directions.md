# Lagged EEG Power Mostly Tracks Arousal State

The VU results are best read as a cross-subject readout of a slow, brain-wide arousal state seen through the haemodynamic delay, not as a map of regional neurovascular coupling, and the EEG-fMRI literature supports every part of that reading: the ridge kernel (alpha and beta power 5–12 s before a frame predicting lower BOLD) is the inverse alpha–BOLD coupling reported since 2002, delayed by an HRF that peaks near 6 s; the vigilance index is the field's standard EEG vigilance measure; and the link between scan predictability and theta-power fluctuation (**ρ = +0.66**) almost exactly replicates a published **r = 0.63**. Because one shared arousal factor dominates every region's prediction, the predicted series are nearly rank one, which is why predicted connectivity never beats the population average. Two things are not yet established: whether the 0.381 headline survives a mismatched-subject null and nested confound models (sub-0.2 mm head motion alters sub-20 Hz EEG and maps onto superior temporal and medial parietal BOLD, where the best regions sit), and how much region-specific BOLD remains predictable once the global signal is removed, a number no published EEG-to-BOLD prediction study reports. In 12–30-person cohorts, tuned linear models match recurrent, convolutional and transformer models wherever both were run; the only same-dataset evidence for nonlinearity is a 2026 preprint (MLP ensemble 0.499 against ridge 0.439, no significance tests), and Google's TimesFM and Amazon's Chronos-2 ignore or attenuate lagged covariate effects, so plausible nonlinear gains lie in vigilance-state gating and subject-specific HRF timing rather than in bigger encoders. About **178 people** with open simultaneous in-scanner rest exist across seven datasets, led by Penn State ds003768 and Magdeburg ds005795. The recommended programme runs eight experiments with explicit falsifiers, beginning with null and confound controls and ending with one interpretable architecture, a shared-factor Hammerstein distributed-lag model with per-region HRF kernels, confirmed on a locked external dataset; throughout, peer-reviewed work is cited with its venue and preprints, abstracts and technical reports are labelled as such.

## Ridge weights are filters, but the kernel they encode is textbook physiology

These models should be read as cross-validated predictive associations whose raw weights are extraction filters. The interpretable objects are activation patterns, the unique variance of feature groups measured by refitting without them, and lag profiles that can be compared with HRF estimates; none of these licenses causal or source-localisation claims. Read this way, the project's kernel is a rediscovery of known physiology, and its one anomalous feature, a short-lag term of opposite sign, has three testable explanations.

### Read patterns and group refits, not individual weights

Haufe et al.'s distinction between filters and patterns applies directly, for a reason that is easy to miss: forward and backward are defined relative to the multivariate measured data, so a ridge that reads one BOLD target out of 13,728 correlated EEG features is a backward extraction filter, and its weights can be large or sign-flipped on features that merely cancel shared variance (*NeuroImage* 2014; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24239590)). Wilming, Haufe and colleagues built linear ground truth containing suppressor variables and found that LRP, SHAP, LIME and permutation methods all assigned importance to the suppressors, while Haufe-style activation patterns gave the best explanations (*Machine Learning* 2022; [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.07473)). For a single target the pattern is cheap, **a = Cov(X, ŷ)/Var(ŷ)**, which is each feature's structure coefficient rescaled by its standard deviation and costs one matrix–vector product per region on held-out predictions ([Kraha et al., *Front Psychol* 2012](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00044)). Because log power is strongly autocorrelated across 2 s lags, the pattern will be much smoother over lag than the weights, and a lobe that appears in the weights but not in the pattern is a filtering device rather than a physiological response. Kriegeskorte and Douglas caution that encoding weights are hard to read when predictors are correlated and penalised (*Curr Opin Neurobiol* 2019; [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6705607/)), and Weichwald et al. show that encoding-model weights support causal statements only when the predictor is experimentally manipulated, which spontaneous EEG power is not (*NeuroImage* 2015; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.04780)).

The question of what the model uses should be answered with group-level, refit-based quantities. Hooker, Mentch and Zhou show that permute-and-predict importance and partial-dependence plots grossly over-weight correlated features because breaking the correlations forces extrapolation, and they recommend refitting without the features (*Stat Comput* 2021; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03151)); leave-one-covariate-out and nonparametric variable-importance frameworks formalise this with valid inference ([Lei et al., *JASA* 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.04173); [Williamson et al., *Biometrics* 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/biom.13392)). Banded ridge, with one penalty per feature space, sets aside redundant spaces and yields a variance decomposition that scales linearly with the number of spaces (Dupré la Tour et al., *NeuroImage* 2022; [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9807218/)). The project's nested models (a 12-feature vigilance index, 60 channel-averaged band powers, and the full 13,728-feature model) already form a drop-group decomposition and are the strongest interpretive result in hand ([project report](NeuroBOLT%20hypothesis%20tests.md)). Redundancy between bands should be expected: de Munck et al. found resting beta–BOLD maps resembled alpha maps, and entering the other bands as confounders changed the maps substantially (*NeuroImage* 2009; [UvA record](https://www.dare.uva.nl/id/b7151024-b98f-4d70-834e-984824ebb377)), so large beta weights can coexist with little unique beta variance.

### The 5–12 s alpha/beta lobe reproduces two decades of EEG-informed fMRI

In the project's ridge maps, more alpha (8–13 Hz) and beta (15–25 Hz) power 5–12 s before a frame predicts lower BOLD in the cuneus, Heschl's gyrus, thalamus and global signal ([project report](NeuroBOLT%20hypothesis%20tests.md)), and both sign and timing match the literature. Eyes-closed alpha couples negatively with lateral frontal and parietal cortex (Laufs et al., *PNAS* 2003, left lateral prefrontal peak Z = 5.8; [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC196925)). De Munck et al. estimated alpha response functions with no assumed shape in 16 people at rest and found occipital and parietal responses similar in amplitude and timing (*NeuroImage* 2007; [UvA record](https://dare.uva.nl/id/2cb87dac-6040-4c67-b6fb-b4cf8a3eb6df)). Scheeringa et al. showed that alpha and beta power each decrease BOLD while high gamma increases it, as independent contributions (*Neuron* 2011; [MPI record](https://forms.mpi.nl/node/46580)), and a laminar follow-up assigned the negative beta effect to deep layers and gamma to superficial layers (*PNAS* 2016; [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4914170)). In eyes-open rest, a preprint by Prokopiou et al. found all bands contributing with region-dependent weights, with HRFs positive in sensorimotor cortex but negative in parieto-occipital cortex ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.06.29.178483v2)). On timing, Taylor, Kim and Ress measured a **time-to-peak of 6.1 ± 0.6 s**, a full width at half maximum of 4.1 ± 0.5 s and an undershoot at 10.8 ± 1.1 s in 20 adults (*NeuroImage* 2018; [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5911213/)), and EEG-derived HRFs peak around 5–10 s (Mann-Krzisnik & Mitsis, *Hum Brain Mapp* 2022; [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9374895/)). That the project's ridge peaks with 24–32 s of history and gains nothing from 48–64 s is exactly what a kernel spanning peak and undershoot predicts.

What scalp EEG cannot see is just as informative. Invasive work places the positive side of neurovascular coupling in gamma: in macaque rest, upper gamma leads BOLD by **about 6–8 s** while 2–15 Hz power couples at near-zero lag (Schölvinck et al., *PNAS* 2010; [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2890714)); 20–60 Hz LFP explains most BOLD variance in awake V1 (Goense & Logothetis, *Curr Biol* 2008; [eprint](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/85078)); and gamma is the most informative band, with alpha and beta complementary (Magri et al., *J Neurosci* 2012; [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3240306/)). The VU features stop at 45 Hz and scalp gamma is muscle-prone, so the model is confined to the negative alpha/beta side of the neurovascular code plus relative spectral shape. Rosa et al. found that a root-mean-square-frequency heuristic, which measures relative high- versus low-frequency power, explained far more BOLD than total power or a three-band model (**620 activated voxels against 5 and 18**; *NeuroImage* 2010; [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2793371/)). On log power such ratios are linear contrasts, which is one structural reason a linear model on log power is hard to beat.

### Three rival explanations for the short-lag opposite-sign term

Power in the last 0–4 s before a frame often carries the opposite sign to the 5–12 s lobe, and three explanations predict different signatures. The first is a suppressor or temporal-derivative device: a weight pair that is positive early and negative late on an autocorrelated regressor acts like a canonical HRF plus its temporal derivative, shifting the effective latency; Rosa et al. found the temporal derivative explained significant variance, and Magri et al. tie beta to BOLD latency rather than amplitude. This predicts that the lobe shrinks or vanishes in the Haufe pattern. The second is a common-cause arousal transient. Broadband 2–20 Hz EEG power co-fluctuates with the global signal at zero lag at rest and rises about 4 s after arousal events, simultaneously with the global-signal peak, a heart-rate rise and finger vasoconstriction; Bolt et al. attribute this to a shared subcortical–autonomic driver rather than EEG driving BOLD through an HRF (*Nat Neurosci* 2025; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-01945-y); quantitative details from the [bioRxiv version](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.01.19.524818v3.full)). This predicts a broadband, spatially diffuse early lobe that survives in the pattern and is strongest for the global signal and sensory cortex. The third is earlier positive thalamic or cingulo-opercular coupling: de Munck et al. found thalamic alpha responses mainly positive and peaking a few seconds before cortex, and Sadaghiani et al. report alpha correlating positively with a cingulo-opercular/thalamic alertness network (*J Neurosci* 2010; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1004-10.2010)). This predicts an alpha-specific early lobe confined to thalamic and insular targets. De Munck's immediate correlations at the eyeball add an ocular-artefact variant with a frontal topography.

The thalamus needs its own caution. Posterior alpha correlates negatively with pulvinar BOLD but positively with the anterior and mediodorsal nuclei (Liu et al., *NeuroImage* 2012; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2012.08.025)), so a DiFuMo thalamic component spanning both should average kernels of opposite sign. The project's numbers fit that picture: the vigilance index predicts the thalamus at only **0.086**, against 0.328 for five channel-averaged band powers ([project report](NeuroBOLT%20hypothesis%20tests.md)), so the thalamic target relies on band-specific weights that a single alertness ratio cannot express. A preprint using subject-specific networks found the thalamus had the shortest haemodynamic delay of seven subcortical targets (Semenkov et al.; [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.11.20.624528v1)). NeuroBOLT's emphasis on reconstructing the thalamus from 26 scalp channels, at a cross-subject R of 0.453 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05341v3)), is better read as coupling with the arousal network than as source-localised deep activity.

### Subject HRF variability, not regional HRF shape, limits pooling

Twelve free 2 s lags span the peak and undershoot of essentially every reported HRF, so misspecified HRF shape per region is absorbed automatically, which is one reason the lagged ridge is hard to beat. What pooling cannot absorb is between-person timing. Time-to-peak ranged from about 2.7 to 6.2 s across 32 subjects, with much smaller variation within a subject (Aguirre et al. 1998, known here only through a [secondary summary](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/neuro.09.008.2009/pdf)); HRFs vary across people and across regions within a person (Handwerker et al., *NeuroImage* 2004; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15050587/)); and Taylor et al. found **27 ± 9% of strongly activated cortex responded negatively**. One kernel per region fitted to 22 people is a blurred average, which predicts smoother, wider pooled kernels than any individual's, and no study has measured how much held-out EEG-to-BOLD accuracy that blur costs. HRF variability also contaminates connectivity, changing simulated functional connectivity by up to 50% and producing a 30.5% mean and 11.5% median connectivity error in 47 people at 7T (Rangaprakash et al., *Magn Reson Med* 2018; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.27146)). Lag differences between regions in the kernel therefore cannot be read as neural timing differences, a point de Munck et al. made about their own data.

Free lags beat imposed shapes. In the MEL preprint's ridge ablations, canonical-HRF-weighted aggregation scored 0.325 average R against 0.452 for free 2 s lag coordinates on the VU data; that 0.452 baseline equals MEL's intra-scan ridge, so the ablations appear to use its intra-scan protocol (preprint, Aug 2026; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.29304v1)). A Lisbon thesis likewise found band powers without a predefined HRF predicted best ([IST record](https://scholar.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/records/8eZN1sanyvnes1GgypOO76MCihkCizfJE48j)). Low-rank separable models were adopted for interpretability rather than accuracy. Martinez-Montes et al.'s N-PLS found an alpha atom with parieto-occipital, thalamic and insular loadings (*NeuroImage* 2004; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15219575)); Van Eyndhoven et al. used region-specific Toeplitz HRFs to localise ictal onset in 10 of 12 patients (*NeuroImage* 2021; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14185)); and Mann-Krzisnik and Mitsis's Laguerre-basis CP model reached **within-subject resting correlations of only 0.19–0.32**, rejecting the null in 1 of 33 cases. The project's own per-region low-rank tensor regression scored 0.495 against ridge's 0.525 on the authors' split (0.328 against 0.385 on validation), so hard rank constraints have cost accuracy here.

### NeuroBOLT is best read as a band-power model with extra parameters

The project's ablations settle most of the deep-model question before any attribution is run: the spectral branch alone scores 0.461 against 0.462 for the full model, the LaBraM branch alone scores 0.024 and never learns under the paper's recipe, and averaging NeuroBOLT with the best ridge leaves held-out R unchanged ([project report](NeuroBOLT%20hypothesis%20tests.md)). What remains is whether the STFT branch implements a band-power map, and the attribution literature says how to ask. Gradient methods with principled baselines (Integrated Gradients, DeepLIFT, LRP) perform best on EEG benchmarks but are inconsistent across individual samples (Cui et al., *Front Comput Neurosci* 2023; [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10470463/)); removing inputs without retraining confounds importance with distribution shift (ROAR, NeurIPS 2019; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10758)); attention weights are not explanations (Jain & Wallace, NAACL 2019; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10186)); and a DFT "virtual inspection layer" reads relevance out per frequency (Vielhaben et al., *Pattern Recognition* 2024; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06365)). LaBraM-specific interpretability exists only as 2026 preprints on classification, including a causal feature audit, listed as a NeurIPS 2026 poster, in which frequency-domain features dominated ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.11410)). The staged `scripts/interp_attribution.py` is the in-distribution design these papers recommend: it swaps a band, scalp region or time segment with the same part from at least 60 s away in the same scan, and fits a ridge surrogate to NeuroBOLT's own predictions. A surrogate that reproduces NeuroBOLT's held-out output closely would settle the question.

## Arousal carries most of the signal, and regional specificity is still unmeasured

Most predictable BOLD in these data is arousal-linked global signal, and EEG-side spatial detail adds little. The BOLD-side question, how much region-specific signal survives removal of the global component, has not been answered here or anywhere in the literature. Motion, muscle and autonomic physiology can each imitate EEG–BOLD coupling, and the decisive null for a cross-subject claim is a mismatched subject.

### Spatially non-specific spectra account for about 87% of explained variance

"Over half the predictable signal" is true in correlation units but overstates the vigilance index in variance units. Squaring the mean R of the project's nested models gives approximate shares of the full model's explained variance (approximate because the square of a mean R is not a mean R²).

| Ridge, 5-fold subject-grouped CV, 29 scans | Features | Mean R | Approx. share of the full model's explained variance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vigilance index, alpha/(delta+theta) × 12 lags | 12 | 0.210 | ≈31% |
| Five channel-averaged band powers × 12 lags | 60 | 0.354 | ≈87% |
| 26 channels × 44 one-Hz bins × 12 lags | 13,728 | 0.379 | 100% |

A single alertness ratio therefore explains about a third of what the full model explains, spatially uniform spectra explain nearly nine-tenths, and topography plus fine frequency resolution add about 0.025 R ([project report](NeuroBOLT%20hypothesis%20tests.md)). These numbers measure EEG-side spatial specificity, not BOLD-side regional specificity: channel-averaged spectra can still predict region-specific BOLD, as the thalamus result shows.

The vigilance literature predicts this pattern closely. Alpha over delta+theta power is the field's standard EEG vigilance measure (Falahpour et al., *NeuroImage* 2018; [bioRxiv full text](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/234179v2.full); Liu & Falahpour, *Front Neurosci* 2020; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2020.00321)), and global-signal amplitude correlates negatively with it in eyes-closed rest (Wong et al., *NeuroImage* 2013; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23899724/)). Across 1,147 resting datasets, **about a third of subjects lost wakefulness within 3 min and half within 10 min** (Tagliazucchi & Laufs, *Neuron* 2014; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.03.020)), so 20-min eyes-closed VU scans should contain large vigilance excursions. Falahpour et al. found that how well their fMRI template tracked EEG vigilance in each run correlated with the amplitude of that run's vigilance fluctuations at r = 0.63 with eyes closed; the project's ρ = +0.66 between scan R and theta-power SD is a near-exact replication, and it explains why folds range from 0.23 to 0.52 and why fold 4's people stay near 0.23 whatever the training size. Between-person variability is the norm: in an early study only 3 of 7 subjects showed the canonical negative alpha–BOLD pattern (Gonçalves et al., *NeuroImage* 2006; [record](https://pure.amsterdamumc.nl/en/publications/correlating-the-alpha-rhythm-to-bold-using-simultaneous-eegfmri-i/)).

Bolt et al. supply a mechanism and a reference scale. One low-frequency spatiotemporal pattern links the global signal, EEG and autonomic signals and persists under end-tidal CO₂ clamping (spatial r = 0.95); the global component accounts for **about 25% of low-frequency whole-brain variance**, and a component shared by global signal, EEG and physiology has a mean pairwise r of about 0.31 ([Nat Neurosci](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-01945-y); [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.01.19.524818v3.full)). Reversibly inactivating the nucleus basalis suppressed the global component while sparing network-specific correlations (Turchi et al., *Neuron* 2018; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2018.01.032)), and arousal fluctuations travel as infraslow global waves through cortex, thalamus, striatum and cerebellum (Raut et al., *Sci Adv* 2021; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abf2709)). The arousal component is largest in visual, auditory and sensorimotor cortex, while the thalamus and cingulo-insular cortex move with the opposite sign (Liu & Falahpour 2020), which matches the project's ranking: Heschl's gyrus and the global signal predict best (0.463 and 0.453 in cross-validation) and the putamen worst (0.270).

The BOLD-side number is missing everywhere. No peer-reviewed EEG-to-BOLD prediction study reports accuracy after global-signal regression (GSR) or after partialling out a vigilance index; the closest is the Semenkov preprint, which scores subcortical targets relative to mean haemodynamic activity to avoid common-mode inflation. Candidate region-specific signals exist, since different resting networks carry different band combinations (Mantini et al., *PNAS* 2007; [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1941820)), beta-2 (17–23 Hz) correlates positively with default-mode nodes (Laufs 2003), and thalamic nuclei split by sign. Post-GSR maps must be read relative to the global mean, because GSR shifts EEG-vigilance correlation maps in the positive direction (Falahpour, summarised in [Liu & Falahpour 2020](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2020.00321)); negative residual coupling is therefore not evidence of anticorrelation.

### Near-rank-one predictions explain the connectivity failure

The population-average connectivity matrix already matches each held-out scan at 0.625; ridge reaches 0.61–0.65 and per-region NeuroBOLT 0.60–0.61, while the shared NeuroBOLT model inflates connectivity and drops to 0.497 because its outputs move together ([project report](NeuroBOLT%20hypothesis%20tests.md)). If one shared arousal factor drives most predicted variance in every region, the predicted series are nearly rank one, and their correlation matrix reproduces the global-dominated population connectivity rather than individual network structure. Turchi et al. show network-specific correlations arise from mechanisms separable from the global component, and HRF variability adds further distortion (Rangaprakash 2018). Individual connectivity is therefore a poorly posed target for zero-shot EEG-to-BOLD; the connectivity of GSR-residual signals, or the time course of the global component's amplitude, is better posed.

### Motion, muscle and autonomic arousal can each impersonate coupling

The VU fMRI release underwent multi-echo ICA, motion regression and low-pass filtering below 0.15 Hz, with no physiological regression and no GSR; the EEG was gradient- and BCG-corrected in BrainVision Analyzer, and ECG, EOG and EMG channels were dropped, leaving 26 ([NeuroBOLT](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05341v3)). Whether carbon-wire-loop (CWL) regression was applied is not stated. Head motion is the clearest artefact route: even sub-0.2 mm movements alter sub-20 Hz EEG power after standard correction, and EEG–BOLD correlations closely resembled motion–BOLD correlations (Fellner et al., *NeuroImage* 2016; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.03.031)). Band-power and motion-artefact predictors that were uncorrelated with BOLD before HRF convolution correlated strongly with it afterwards in bilateral superior temporal cortex, SMA, medial parietal cortex and cerebellum (Jansen et al., *NeuroImage* 2012; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2011.06.094)). Those are exactly where the best project regions sit, since Heschl's gyrus is superior temporal and the precuneus medial parietal, and among cortical targets Heschl's gains most beyond the vigilance index (0.270 to 0.459). CWL correction outperforms post-processing-only correction (van der Meer et al., *NeuroImage* 2016; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2015.10.064)), and the project's staging restricts CWL analyses to the 19 scans recorded with loops. Muscle is the second route, because temporal electrodes sit over the temporalis; one project result argues against muscle-driven differences between scans, since 30–45 Hz power is unrelated to scan R (ρ = +0.04), although that test cannot exclude muscle bursts within a scan. Respiration is the third: with eyes closed, alpha global field power tracks respiratory volume, alpha and respiration BOLD maps look alike, and physiological regressors reduce but do not abolish alpha–BOLD correlation (Yuan et al., *NeuroImage* 2013; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2013.04.068)), while multi-echo ICA does not remove respiration-related global signals (Power et al., *PNAS* 2018; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1720985115)).

The harder problem is that every physiological channel is itself coupled to arousal. Heart rate rises and finger pulse amplitude falls at arousals (Bolt 2025); drops in finger vascular tone accompany K-complexes and widespread fMRI decreases (Özbay et al., *Commun Biol* 2019; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-019-0659-0)); fMRI–autonomic covariation strengthens as vigilance falls (Gold et al., *Imaging Neurosci* 2024; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/imag_a_00287)); spontaneous eye movements with eyes closed couple to resting fMRI (Ramot et al., *NeuroImage* 2011; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2011.06.015)); DVARS and motion metrics partly index arousal events (Gu, Han & Liu, preprint; [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/444463.full.pdf)); and cardiac and respiratory response functions differ across people (Kassinopoulos & Mitsis, *NeuroImage* 2019; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2019.116150)). Regressing these out of BOLD removes real arousal signal along with artefact. The defensible design separates true artefact channels (CWL, EMG) from arousal co-markers (heart rate, slow EOG), then compares nested cross-validated models (nuisance-only, EEG-only, and both with separate penalty scales) and reports EEG's unique contribution. The precise claim for an advisor is that the model reads arousal state, a real brain state expressed in BOLD through both neural and autonomic-vascular routes, which is the interpretation Bolt et al. adopt.

### The decisive null is a mismatched subject, not a shuffled frame

Autocorrelation badly miscalibrates naive tests: Afyouni, Smith and Nichols found naive false-positive rates approaching 50%, with about 35% of regions "significant" in scrambled null data against 2.6% under their xDF correction (*NeuroImage* 2019; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2019.05.011)). An in-house simulation matching the VU preprocessing (1/f BOLD, low-pass below 0.15 Hz, independent HRF-convolved band power, 4,000 pairs) gave a null 95th-percentile |r| of **0.20 for a 20-min scan and 0.35 for a 2-min segment**, but only about 0.04 for the mean of 22 independent scans. Sampling noise cannot explain a cross-subject mean of 0.381; shared non-specific structure can. A mismatched-subject (session-permutation) null applies the trained model to person A's EEG and scores it against person B's time-aligned BOLD, preserving everything locked to time-in-scan that the 22 people share: a drift towards drowsiness, onset transients, filter edges and detrending. The effect is the matched-minus-mismatched gap, and slow drifts create spurious correlations that session permutation detects (Elber-Dorozko & Loewenstein, *eLife* 2018; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.34248); Harris, preprint; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1101/2020.11.29.402719)).

Three secondary nulls complement it. Circular shifts beyond the lag window plus HRF (30–60 s) preserve both autocorrelations but assume a stationarity that drowsiness drift violates. Multivariate phase-randomised or IAAFT surrogates of the EEG feature matrix, with shared phases across features, test whether temporal alignment matters beyond spectral content (Prichard & Theiler, *Phys Rev Lett* 1994; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.73.951); review in Lancaster et al., *Phys Rep* 2018; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physrep.2018.06.001)). xDF or Pyper–Peterman corrections, using the predicted series' autocorrelation as well as the BOLD's, give per-scan p-values. Group tests should treat people rather than frames as units. NeuroBOLT's intra-scan scores come from roughly 2-min test windows with a null spread near ±0.35, so they are not comparable with cross-subject scores on full scans, and temporally adjacent splits leak (Mehta et al., preprint; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10752)). No EEG-fMRI paper reports a mismatched-subject null for cross-subject prediction, so running one is itself a contribution.

## Nonlinearity earns its place only in narrow niches

In cohorts of 12–30 people, deep models beat linear ones only where the linear baseline was weak or unspecified, and tied wherever it was regularised and tuned. Nonlinearity has a principled job in three niches (vigilance-state gating, subject-specific HRF timing and more data per person), while state-space models, time-series foundation models and EEG foundation-model swaps each target a constraint that is not binding here.

Wider benchmarks agree. In Algonauts 2025, with about 80 h of fMRI per subject, a fourth-placed team using only temporal convolution and a linear projection reported that one more hour of work would have placed it second, and ensembling rather than architecture separated the ranks (winners' analysis, preprint; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10784v1)). Linear models matched kernel and deep models for UK Biobank age and sex prediction up to about 10,000 subjects (Schulz et al., *Nat Commun* 2020; [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7447816/)). Where nonlinearity did win big, as in VLAAI's subject-independent EEG speech decoder at **+52% over linear**, it trained on about 80 subjects along a saturating learning curve (Accou et al., *Sci Rep* 2023; [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-27332-2)). Banville et al. found the deep-learning gain over linear models largest for the noisiest devices but scaling mainly with data per subject, with little gain from adding subjects (preprint 2025; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15322)). The project's own evidence points the same way: boosted trees score below ridge (0.412 and 0.454), and ridge's learning curve plateaus by about nine training people.

MEL is the one same-dataset result for nonlinearity and deserves replication rather than dismissal. Its uniform-STFT ridge (0.382) is almost identical to the project's 0.381, which makes its protocol credible, but three discrepancies need checking. Its NeuroBOLT figure (0.473) equals the paper's single-split number, suggesting reuse rather than a leave-one-subject-out re-run; its five-band cube beats the STFT grid under ridge (0.439 against 0.382), whereas the project found 1 Hz bins beat five bands (0.497 against 0.469 on the authors' split); and its figures were machine-extracted from the HTML, its MLP size is unreported, and it reports no significance tests. Its validity ablations do independently confirm the project's picture, since lag reversal drops ridge to 0.102 and band reversal to 0.236 while channel permutation only drops it to 0.395, so band × lag structure carries the signal rather than topography. The staged `scripts/interp_nonlinear.py` tests MEL's claim under the project's protocol, including within-scan z-scoring, which may be part of MEL's preprocessing and which lowered the project's ridge from 0.497 to 0.474.

### Vigilance gating, subject HRF timing and per-person data are where nonlinearity can pay

State-dependent coupling is the first mechanism. VIGALL staging describes posterior alpha at full wakefulness that shifts forward as vigilance declines, vanishes into low-amplitude non-alpha EEG at stage B1, and gives way to theta and delta at B2/3 (Sander et al., *Neuropsychobiology* 2015; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1159/000439384); Olbrich et al., *NeuroImage* 2009; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2008.11.014)). Low alpha therefore means opposite things at the two ends of the vigilance axis, and coupling strength itself changes with state: brain–body coupling strengthens as vigilance falls (Gold 2024), eyes-closed and eyes-open vigilance maps differ (Falahpour 2018), and macaque LFP–fMRI coupling is stronger and anticipatory with eyes closed (Schölvinck 2010). Band × vigilance products or monotone splines capture this with a handful of parameters, and an MLP learns it implicitly. Subject-specific HRF timing is the second mechanism and is nonlinear in the parameters: a latency shift of a few seconds against a pooled kernel cannot be fixed by reweighting a new person's EEG, and without that person's BOLD it cannot be estimated at all, so it pays only in a calibration protocol that uses a few minutes of the target's fMRI (in its own domain, VLAAI gained up to 34% from five minutes of a new subject's data). Data per person is the third: sleep and multi-session datasets add hours per person and a wider arousal range, which Banville's scaling result identifies as the regime in which deep models start to pay, rather than more 20-min resting scans.

### State-space models, filterbanks, HRF layers and foundation models mostly miss the bottleneck

State-space models such as S4 and Mamba earn their keep on long contexts at linear cost, but 24 s of history in 2 s lags is twelve steps, so that advantage is not binding. EEG Mamba work targets classification or foundation-model pretraining (State Mamba, AAAI 2026, [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/38843); EEGM2, preprint, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.17873v1)), and the only EEG-to-BOLD state-space model, MARBLE, cannot yet be inspected. Learnable filterbanks such as SincNet ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.00158)) and FBCNet ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01233)) have no published EEG-to-BOLD use, and because 1 Hz log-power bins already beat five fixed bands, a learned spectral profile on 1 Hz bins subsumes most of what a filterbank would add; the closest deep analogue, R-EEGNet's learned depthwise filters, only matched group lasso. Physics-informed HRF layers should mean a constrained smooth basis, whether Laguerre (Mann-Krzisnik & Mitsis), Toeplitz (Van Eyndhoven) or canonical plus derivatives (Rosa), rather than a fixed canonical shape, which cost about 0.13 R in MEL's ablation; deep transcoding models that learn the HRF from data recover canonical-like timing with clear differences between subjects (NeurIPS 2021, [proceedings page](https://nips.cc/virtual/2021/36818); preprint extension, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.02226)).

Time-series foundation models are mismatched to a problem that is exogenous-input system identification with a 4–10 s lag. Google Research's TimesFM 2.5 handles covariates through XReg, a linear regression of the model's residuals on the covariates ([TimesFM docs](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/google-research/timesfm)). A single-author October 2026 preprint found the covariate interfaces of TimesFM-2.5 and TabPFN-TS memoryless, with the predicted effect of an input change depending only on the same-time change (R² = 1.000), while Amazon's Chronos-2 (itself a preprint; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15821v1)) recovers dynamics but predicts **only 0.33–0.80 of the true effect** with a wrong-shaped impulse response; on a one-degree-of-freedom oscillator Chronos-2's error plateaus at 0.57 even with 8,192 context samples, while an ARX model fitted to 256 samples reaches 0.02 (Won, preprint; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08118)). A lagged ridge is an ARX model. The one frontier tool with a plausible role is TabPFN-2.5 used as a plain tabular regressor on explicitly lagged features: it is built for up to 50,000 rows and 2,000 features (technical report; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.08667)), the VU data are roughly 16,500 frames, and the band cube has 1,560 features, which makes it a cheap nonlinear benchmark provided its in-context set excludes the test person. No OpenAI or Anthropic time-series or neural-data model was found.

EEG foundation models hit the same small-data wall. An ICLR 2026 evaluation found that fine-tuned EEG foundation models often fail to beat compact or classical decoders when labels are scarce and that linear probes are consistently weak ([ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10011450)); two 2026 benchmark preprints agree ([OmniEEG-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00815v1); [EEG-FM-Compass](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17883v1)); and the project's LaBraM branch contributed nothing. Small-data techniques with measured effects belong in training, because a zero-shot protocol has no target-person labels: subject-specific layers (removing one cut EEG top-10 accuracy from 17.7% to 8.1%; Défossez et al., *Nat Mach Intell* 2023; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.12266v2)), Euclidean alignment (+4.33%; *J Neural Eng* 2024; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10746v4)) and seed ensembles.

## Eight falsifiable experiments, ordered by what they protect

The order follows one rule: protect the headline before interpreting it, and interpret it before building on it. Experiments 1–5 run on cached VU features and are mostly scripted already in `scripts/interp_cv.py`, `interp_maps.py`, `interp_nonlinear.py` and `interp_attribution.py`; experiments 6 and 8 need new code; and experiment 7 needs data preparation, which is the long pole and should start now in parallel. All use the 5-fold subject-grouped split, choose hyperparameters on validation people, report paired per-person ΔR with intervals over people, freeze thresholds before results are read, and hold back ds005795 as the locked confirmatory set.

Read as a set, experiments 1 and 3 put a number on how much of the predictable signal is arousal versus regional, experiment 2 tests the motion account, experiment 4 settles how the kernels should be interpreted, experiments 5 and 6 test whether nonlinearity earns its place, and experiment 7 turns the dataset ranking into a result. Every outcome is informative. Residual predictability at null in every region would be a strong negative result showing that resting-state EEG-to-fMRI models learn vigilance; no MLP gain under the project's protocol would mark MEL's result as a protocol artefact and strengthen the case for linear models at this scale; and a short-lag lobe surviving as a broadband global component would mean the model reads arousal transients as they happen rather than through neurovascular delay.

### A shared-factor Hammerstein model is the architecture to test

The interpretable architecture to test is a shared-factor Hammerstein distributed-lag model, in which a few spatial–spectral components are shared across all target regions, each passes through an optional static nonlinearity (the Hammerstein stage), and each feeds every region through its own smooth lag kernel over 0–24 s, which serves as an EEG-informed HRF.

```
s_k(t)   = Σ_c Σ_f  a_kc · b_kf · logP_cf(t)                    k = 1..K shared components
ŷ_r(t)   = β_r + Σ_k Σ_ℓ  h_rk(ℓ − δ_s) · g_k( s_k(t − ℓ) )      ℓ = 0..24 s in 2 s steps
h_rk(ℓ)  = Σ_j θ_rkj · φ_j(ℓ)                                   φ_j: 5 Laguerre or B-spline functions
g_k      = identity (linear arm) or monotone spline (nonlinear arm); δ_s = per-person shift, training only
```

Each a_k is a 26-channel scalp topography and each b_k a 44-bin spectral profile, so the components are atoms in Martinez-Montes's sense, and the region-by-component kernels follow the Laguerre-basis HRFs of Mann-Krzisnik and Mitsis, the Toeplitz regional HRFs of Van Eyndhoven, and Prokopiou's block-structured models, which convolve an HRF with a combination of band powers (preprint). A per-person latency shift δ_s, shrunk towards zero, de-blurs the pooled kernel during training; at test it is zero for zero-shot prediction, or fitted on calibration minutes for experiment 8. With K = 4 and 65 targets the model has about 280 shared and 1,300 kernel parameters, roughly 1,600 in all, against about 892,000 ridge weights (13,728 × 65), and it can be fitted by alternating least squares with banded penalties followed by gradient refinement of the splines.

Four reasons make this the right test. Every parameter maps onto a concept the EEG-informed fMRI literature already uses (topography, spectral profile, response nonlinearity, regional HRF), so its outputs answer the advisor's questions directly: component 1's share of variance in each region is the arousal share, its kernels can be compared with Taylor's HRF statistics, and the thalamic kernel's latency tests de Munck's early thalamic peak. It nests the project's working models, since K = 1 with uniform spatial weights and an alpha/(delta+theta) spectral profile is the vigilance model, and K = 5 with uniform spatial weights approximates the 60-feature model that already reaches 93% of ridge. It differs from the per-region tensor regression that lost 0.03 R, because its components are shared across all 65 targets, pooling strength along the shared arousal axis, while each component keeps its own regional kernel so that alpha and beta can take the different latencies Magri et al. describe. And its spline arm is the minimal nonlinearity matching the vigilance-gating hypothesis: if experiment 5's MLP gain is real and the splines capture most of it, the nonlinearity is a static gain change, whereas if the MLP wins and the splines do not, the nonlinearity involves interactions between components. The alternatives each fail on evidence already in hand: Mamba-style models address a context length that is not binding, time-series foundation models ignore or attenuate lags, EEG foundation-model swaps have negative small-data evidence, and end-to-end raw-EEG networks failed in the LaBraM branch.

The hypothesis is that the predictable part of resting BOLD in these data is low-dimensional and separable, so K ≤ 5 shared components with regional kernels lose at most 0.01 R against full ridge. It is falsified by a loss above 0.02, which would mean ridge's advantage lies in unstructured, partly suppressor-like detail, and that interpretive claims must come from Haufe patterns of the full model rather than from a compact one.

## Conclusion

The project has not built a weaker or a stronger NeuroBOLT; it has measured what scalp EEG can tell about a new person's BOLD, and so far the answer is mainly where that person sits on the arousal axis, delayed by an HRF. That makes the arousal variance available in each scan, rather than model capacity or cohort size, the ceiling. It predicts that more 20-min resting scans will help little and that longer, sleepier recordings will help more, and it sets the bar for any deep model at a paired per-person win over a tuned lagged ridge on a locked external dataset, a standard most 2024–2026 EEG-to-fMRI papers do not yet meet.

The most valuable unknown is the BOLD-side residual: how much region-specific signal EEG predicts once the global component is removed, scored against a mismatched-subject null. Either answer is publishable. A null residual would show that resting-state EEG-to-fMRI translation models, NeuroBOLT included, are vigilance decoders, and would redirect effort towards task data and calibrated per-person models. A positive residual concentrated in thalamic nuclei, cingulo-opercular cortex or default-mode nodes would make two decades of within-subject EEG-informed fMRI findings testable across people for the first time, with region-specific HRF kernels from an interpretable model as the deliverable rather than another leaderboard number.
