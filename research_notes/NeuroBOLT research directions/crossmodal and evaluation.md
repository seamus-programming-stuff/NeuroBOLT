# EEG-to-BOLD methods and evaluation

Scope: primary papers, official proceedings/publishers, official datasets and code available through 4 October 2026. Claims from recent work are distinguished from established methodological evidence; newer papers with restricted data or incomplete reproducibility are treated as research leads rather than settled best practice.

## What newer methods can improve EEG-to-BOLD beyond fixed windows and one-ROI-at-a-time regression?

### Takeaway

The most evidence-backed near-term change is to represent the neural-to-hemodynamic mapping as a lagged, region-dependent temporal process, and to predict multiple ROIs jointly. NeuroBOLT already extracts temporal and multiscale spectral EEG features, so the clearest extension is not simply “add a transformer”: it is to make the input-to-BOLD lag inspectable and learnable, share an encoder across regions, and test whether predicted time series preserve functional connectivity as well as pointwise values.

### Cited Findings

- NeuroBOLT’s model combines a LaBraM-derived spatiotemporal Transformer branch and a multiscale spectral branch, then adds the embeddings and applies a scalar ROI regression head. In the current code/configuration, training is launched one ROI at a time; the paired samples are a 16-second EEG epoch (3,200 samples at 200 Hz) and one BOLD frame. The official paper describes 29 resting scans from 22 volunteers, 7 of whom contributed two scans, each scan about 20 minutes long, with 26 retained scalp channels and DiFuMo parcellations. [NeuroBOLT paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05341); [official code](https://github.com/soupeeli/NeuroBOLT/blob/main/models/model.py); [official dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/NeurdyLab/NeuroBOLT)
- Transfer-function work predating deep models provides a clear physiological reason to avoid one fixed lag/HRF assumption: Rosa et al. compared total-power, band-specific and joint frequency-response models and found the EEG–BOLD relationship depended on the spectral profile, particularly relative high- versus low-frequency power. Sato et al. introduced a data-driven EEG-to-BOLD transfer-function method that avoids downsampling EEG to fMRI rate and avoids committing to one haemodynamic response shape. [Rosa et al. (2010)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2793371/); [Sato et al. (2010)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053811910000972)
- Simultaneous EEG-fMRI experiments have found the EEG/BOLD relationship and estimated HRF can vary with region and brain state. A study spanning visual, resting, and movie conditions reported state- and region-dependent EEG-band/BOLD coupling, warning that a single canonical HRF may bias coupling estimates. The evidence supports allowing flexible lag/transfer functions, but it does not imply that every ROI or person needs an unconstrained neural HRF estimator. [Butler et al., ISMRM 2018 proceedings](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/18MProceedings/PDFfiles/4512.html)
- A 2026 MIDL paper, UnEBOLT, directly targets a current NeuroBOLT limitation: one adaptive multi-region model predicts whole-brain time series and reconstructs subject-specific functional-connectivity matrices. Its proceedings abstract reports performance comparable to dedicated ROI-specific models while scaling to multi-region prediction. This is a strong architecture to reproduce/benchmark; the PMLR page does not link a public implementation, and the abstract alone is insufficient to verify exact data splits, effect sizes, or external replication. [UnEBOLT, MIDL 2026 / PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v315/li26g.html)
- A 2026 MICCAI paper explores an adjacent target: translate EEG-derived FC to fMRI FC using graph-to-graph mapping, unpaired representation learning and a frozen fMRI teacher. The paper’s response reports 115 subjects and 831 paired sessions drawn from six heterogeneous task/rest sources, with a subject-wise 80/12/23-subject split. It explicitly distinguishes FC prediction from moment-by-moment BOLD, and its response acknowledges that evidence for downstream Alzheimer’s classification is supportive rather than definitive and that some statistical/reproducibility details needed clarification. Treat this as evidence that connectivity-level translation and teacher-student alignment are active directions, not as a direct replacement result for pointwise BOLD regression. [MICCAI 2026 open-access paper and author response](https://papers.miccai.org/miccai-2026/0130-Paper0924.html)
- A 2026 Wiley paper proposes a Hierarchical Temporal Attention Network (HTAN): EEG context aligned to fMRI sampling plus hierarchical temporal attention over multiple EEG scales, with ROI-level BOLD output. The abstract claims scan-wise correlation of 0.82 ± 0.02 and cross-subject generalization, but the paper’s data statement says data are available from the corresponding author on request; independent verification and reuse therefore remain limited. It is a useful temporal-architecture hypothesis, not a reliable performance target until the split, dataset, code and baselines can be checked. [Zhang et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpe.70860)
- A 2025 EUSIPCO study proposed R-EEGNet for ROI BOLD regression during motor imagery. On 15 healthy participants, the model used a 15-second EEG segment and was comparable to a group-lasso model on EEG spectral features (median Pearson r around 0.20 for task-evoked and 0.15 for trial-by-trial activity), with both outperforming a simple sensorimotor-rhythm baseline. This supports retaining compact, transparent EEGNet/ridge baselines and testing whether large pretrained encoders add value; it is task-specific, not evidence about eyes-closed resting-state performance. [Stabile et al. (EUSIPCO 2025)](https://eusipco2025.org/wp-content/uploads/pdfs/0001025.pdf)
- CATD (Condition-Aligned Temporal Diffusion) uses condition-aligned blocks and a diffusion Transformer to synthesize fMRI functional maps from EEG and proposes temporal super-resolution. It is a 2025 IEEE TMI paper (preprint available); the authors describe super-resolution experiments where low-resolution observed fMRI is supplied as a condition and report limited paired data. That setting does not establish EEG-only recovery of truly unobserved high-frequency BOLD. Diffusion is consequently a later exploratory direction for this small dataset, not the first experiment to prioritize. [CATD preprint/full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00777); [IEEE TMI publication metadata](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2025.3550206)
- A 2025/2026 author-posted conference paper on asynchronous EEG-to-fMRI mapping changes NeuroBOLT to accept 63 channels, uses 8-second windows, predicts ROI sequences, and adds an intra-ROI correlation-matrix loss. It reports separate-session EEG and fMRI in 13 stroke patients and 14 controls and subject-wise train/validation/test partitions; the small cohorts and the authors’ stated need for future unseen-stroke validation make this promising preliminary support for sequence and connectivity losses, not mature clinical evidence. [Peng et al. paper](https://publish.illinois.edu/ritahtpeng/files/2026/01/IEEE2025_revised__Asynchronous_EEG_to_fMRI_Translation_Using_Multi_dimensional_Feature_Mapping_in_Stroke-3.pdf)

### Inferences

- **First model experiment (feasible now):** use the current EEG encoder, but retain a sequence of latent tokens/features rather than pooling everything to one vector; predict a short BOLD sequence across all 64 DiFuMo regions with a shared decoder and learned ROI embeddings/queries. Compare with current separate-ROI runs, using exactly the same subject split. Report both ROI-wise MSE/correlation and within-scan FC similarity. The dataset already has DiFuMo-64 targets according to its official card, while the current reproduction list only trains seven selected ROIs. [Dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/NeurdyLab/NeuroBOLT); [NeuroBOLT code](https://github.com/soupeeli/NeuroBOLT)
- **Lag/HRF ablation (feasible now):** compare same-length EEG windows ending at the BOLD acquisition marker and windows ending, for example, 2, 4, 6 or 8 seconds earlier; separately compare lookbacks of 8, 16, 24 and 32 seconds. Let training/validation select offsets, then evaluate the chosen configuration once on the held-out subjects. In a second version, use a small per-ROI temporal convolution/FIR layer over EEG-band envelopes or latent tokens, with a mild smoothness/lag prior; inspect the learned lag profiles for anatomical plausibility. These are proposed experiments based on demonstrated spectral/region/state variation, not established winning choices. [Rosa et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2793371/); [Sato et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053811910000972); [Butler et al.](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/18MProceedings/PDFfiles/4512.html)
- **Objective ablation (feasible now):** keep pointwise MSE as a baseline; compare Huber loss, a temporal derivative loss, and a small differentiable FC/correlation loss computed over non-overlapping windows (for example, 30–60-second blocks) alongside MSE. The FC term should be auxiliary rather than the only objective: matching FC alone can yield a plausible covariance structure with badly time-shifted predictions. Evaluate FC on the test scans as a held-out downstream property, not only as a training loss. The sequence-plus-FC objective has recent empirical precedent in the small asynchronous stroke study and multi-region FC is central to UnEBOLT, but neither proves it helps NeuroBOLT’s rest setting. [Peng et al.](https://publish.illinois.edu/ritahtpeng/files/2026/01/IEEE2025_revised__Asynchronous_EEG_to_fMRI_Translation_Using_Multi_dimensional_Feature_Mapping_in_Stroke-3.pdf); [UnEBOLT](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v315/li26g.html)
- **Foundation-model question (feasible now):** compare the current LaBraM initialization/fine-tuning against frozen LaBraM plus a small decoder, scratch EEGNet/TCN, and regularized band-power ridge regression. The relevant scientific question is whether EEG foundation features help prediction across people above simple spectral/temporal baselines, not whether they can fit an individual scan. Published motor-imagery work found group lasso competitive with the deep model, so stronger baselines are necessary. [R-EEGNet study](https://eusipco2025.org/wp-content/uploads/pdfs/0001025.pdf); [NeuroBOLT code](https://github.com/soupeeli/NeuroBOLT)
- **Asynchronous/session-shift and diffusion models** need larger or different paired datasets before they should be interpreted as meaningful improvements. With 29 scans, a 64-ROI diffusion model or a broad hyperparameter search has a high capacity-to-subject ratio; if tried, treat it as feasibility work and validate externally. [NeuroBOLT dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/NeurdyLab/NeuroBOLT); [CATD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00777)

### Gaps

- The latest HTAN abstract gives no data size or detailed split on the accessible page, and its data are request-only; the reported correlation cannot yet be treated as a reproducible benchmark. [HTAN](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpe.70860)
- The PMLR UnEBOLT page provides an abstract but no code link; inspect the full methods/data supplement or contact authors before claiming exact reproducibility or translating its objective. [UnEBOLT](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v315/li26g.html)
- The repo’s public dataset card says it releases resting data with 64 DiFuMo regions and that task-condition data/higher-resolution atlases require contacting the authors. The local M3 reproduction runner is explicitly rest-only, so auditory/task generalization cannot be tested from the current staged data. [Official dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/NeurdyLab/NeuroBOLT); [current runner](../../../scripts/reproduce_rest_m3.sbatch)

## What evaluation practices matter most for autocorrelation, splits, lag, confounds, chance and uncertainty?

### Takeaway

The current split’s subject grouping is valuable, but the number of independent people is small and some preprocessing is estimated/applied over the entire scan before splitting. For a defensible claim, separate temporal generalization from new-subject generalization; fit all learned preprocessing only on training data; keep the test cohort hidden until one final evaluation; and use baselines/nulls and uncertainty intervals that preserve temporal and subject dependence.

### Cited Findings

- The current loader pairs each fMRI sample with the preceding `tmin=-16` to `tmax=0` EEG window at 200 Hz. The intra-scan path uses an ordered first-80% / next-10% / final-10% split and inserts a 20-second gap at both boundaries, corresponding to 10 volumes at TR=2.1 s; this is a sensible guard against shared input windows, since adjacent examples are only 2.1 seconds apart and their 16-second EEG windows overlap heavily. [Current loader](../../../dataset_maker/get_datasets.py); [official code](https://github.com/soupeeli/NeuroBOLT/blob/main/dataset_maker/get_datasets.py)
- The same loader low-pass filters each full BOLD scan with `filtfilt` before splitting and calls `normalize_data` on the full scan; `normalize_data` estimates each ROI’s mean and SD over the full time axis. Thus an intra-scan validation/test period contributes to its own target scale and, because zero-phase filtering is run across the entire scan, filter context crosses split boundaries. This is a code-level audit, not a statement that the reported paper results are invalid; it means the intra-scan score should be distinguished from a strictly prospective estimate. [Current preprocessing](../../../dataset_maker/get_datasets.py); [normalizer and epoching](../../../dataset_maker/preproc.py)
- The cross-scan loader applies EEG high-pass filtering (0.5 Hz) per scan and low-pass BOLD filtering, then normalizes every scan using that scan’s own full BOLD time series before concatenating scans within the train/validation/test groups. Per-scan target z-scoring therefore uses a held-out scan’s target mean and SD; this is a transductive normalization choice and should be made explicit if the intended use is a new person with no BOLD measurements. The intra-scan path does not apply the same explicit EEG high-pass call, so the two protocols should not be described as a single identical preprocessing pipeline. [Current loader](../../../dataset_maker/get_datasets.py); [preprocessing utility](../../../dataset_maker/preproc.py)
- The supplied `scan_split_example.xlsx` does group all scans from one subject into a single partition, matching the official README instruction. Reading the workbook gives 18 train scans from 13 subjects, 5 validation scans from 4 subjects, and 6 test scans from 5 subjects (22 subjects overall; 7 have repeated scans). This is appropriate for a first unseen-subject test but leaves only five test subjects and one fixed split. [README split instruction](https://github.com/soupeeli/NeuroBOLT#configure-the-model-for-your-dataset); [split workbook](../../../scan_split_example.xlsx); [dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/NeurdyLab/NeuroBOLT)
- The current training loop evaluates the test set after every epoch and logs the test statistics; it also gates checkpoint saving on `test_corr > 0`. The reproduction collector chooses the row with minimum validation MSE, which is a validation-based epoch rule, but repeated test visibility and a test-based save condition invite adaptive human/model-selection leakage. For confirmatory reporting, select epoch/configuration only on validation, disable test-dependent checkpoint logic, then run the chosen checkpoint on test once. [Training/evaluation code](https://github.com/soupeeli/NeuroBOLT/blob/main/main.py); [evaluation loop](https://github.com/soupeeli/NeuroBOLT/blob/main/engine.py); [current collector](../../../scripts/collect_reproduction_results.py)
- EEG translational studies can badly overstate generalization when temporally segmented observations from a person appear in both train and test: a primary 2024 analysis found EEG seizure classification accuracy fell from about 79% with segment-wise splitting to about 65% with subject-wise splitting, and surveyed 63 papers, of which only 17 clearly used subject-level separation. A 2024 connectome study likewise showed that feature and subject leakage can inflate neuroimaging prediction, with larger leakage effects at smaller sample sizes. [Brookshire et al. (2024)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2024.1373515/full); [Rosenblatt et al. (2024)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-46150-w)
- A primary neuroimaging confound study found that confound regression can itself bias predictions when estimated outside the CV loop; cross-validated confound regression gave more plausible estimates in its simulations and empirical case. A primary 2024 simulation study of external validation found power depended on both training- and external-sample sizes; even high-effect brain-phenotype predictions needed samples in the hundreds for adequate power, while low/medium effects needed hundreds to thousands. These analyses concern participant-level brain/phenotype models, so they support the evaluation principle but do not directly calculate power for EEG-to-BOLD time-series metrics. [Snoek et al. (2019)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053811918319463); [Rosenblatt et al. (2024)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01931-7)
- A primary comparison of model-selection methods for time-series forecasting recommends blocked evaluation rather than ordinary random CV when temporal order and dependence matter. Primary fMRI studies show slow respiratory-volume variation correlates with BOLD across gray matter and can overlap default-mode regions; RETROICOR demonstrated cardiac- and respiratory-phase-related signal correction using recorded pulse and breathing traces. Thus an EEG-to-BOLD score alone does not establish that the decoder learned only neural coupling. [Bergmeir et al. (2018)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0020025511006773); [Birn et al. (2006)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16632379/); [Glover et al. (2000)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10893535/)
- Standard conformal prediction assumes exchangeability; time dependence violates that assumption. HopCPT and newer 2026 time-series conformal work explicitly address dependent observations with temporal structure/block-based inference. For this project, block/subject-level empirical coverage is more defensible than treating every 2.1-second volume as an independent calibration sample. [Auer et al., NeurIPS 2023](https://research.google/pubs/conformal-prediction-for-time-series-with-modern-hopfield-networks/); [Zhai et al. (2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33868)

### Inferences

- Retain the existing chronological intra-scan experiment as a within-scan/within-person forecast-style result, but report it separately from the grouped cross-subject result. In the intra-scan route, move target scaling and any learned nuisance/confound regression into the train fit, apply learned parameters to val/test, and either use a causal filter for prospective prediction or explicitly label zero-phase filtering as offline retrospective analysis. Use a purge at least as long as the maximum EEG lookback plus an empirically estimated BOLD/filter dependence margin; test the sensitivity of metrics to longer gaps.
- Preserve the current workbook split as the reproduction/confirmation split. For new model development, use grouped outer CV by participant among training participants, with an inner grouped validation loop for model choice; then make the fixed five-person test a one-shot final evaluation. Do not move scans across groups to chase better metrics. With five independent test participants, report subject-wise values and a bootstrap interval over participants, while acknowledging that intervals will be coarse.
- Report per-scan and per-subject Pearson r and MSE, then macro-average by subject (not merely concatenate every frame); include MAE, R² or skill relative to a training-mean prediction, and calibration slope/intercept. Add lagged cross-correlation curves so a high correlation at the wrong delay is visible. Because each scan is normalized separately and frame-level samples are strongly dependent, frame-weighted pooled metrics should be secondary.
- Baseline ladder: (1) training-set mean prediction, (2) previous BOLD frame / AR(1) and smooth/trend-only predictor to measure how much score comes from BOLD autocorrelation, (3) regularized lagged EEG band-power ridge/FIR, (4) EEGNet/TCN or linear EEG baseline, (5) current NeuroBOLT, and (6) proposed multi-ROI variant. Also run a nuisance-only baseline where available. The BOLD-persistence baseline is diagnostic rather than an admissible EEG-only deployment model; it answers whether the apparent performance beats target smoothness.
- For association significance, generate scan-wise circular-shift or phase-randomized EEG surrogates that preserve temporal structure while breaking EEG-BOLD alignment; choose shifts well outside the plausible lag range and repeat training/scoring where computationally feasible. A resting EEG-fMRI study used shuffled and IAAFT surrogate EEG predictors specifically to check whether observed EEG-informed fMRI maps could arise from autocorrelated noise. Avoid iid row shuffles and iid confidence intervals. Report the number of scans/subjects and null distribution, not just nominal Pearson p-values. [Arzate-Mena et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2021.118763)
- The current available recordings are simultaneous, one site, eyes-closed rest. Physiological artifacts, vigilance, motion and scan/session identity can drive shared slow fluctuations. Where ECG/EOG/EMG, respiration, eye-tracker or motion traces are available, compare EEG-only against nuisance-only and EEG-plus-nuisance models; evaluate whether performance changes after train-fold-only nuisance regression. Do not interpret predictive accuracy as a direct measure of neural causality.
- Add uncertainty as a second deliverable rather than a complexity-first architecture change: predict mean and log variance per ROI with Gaussian NLL, or use an ensemble over training seeds; calibrate interval widths on held-out validation blocks/subjects and report empirical coverage versus width. If using conformal intervals, define calibration units as non-overlapping blocks or people and clearly state that classical exchangeability guarantees do not automatically apply to adjacent fMRI samples.
- Test significance across seven ROI hypotheses or multiple lag/window configurations needs a predeclared primary metric/ROI family and correction or a clearly exploratory label; otherwise the search itself overfits the five-person test set. Freeze lags, windows, checkpoints, and ROI comparisons before opening that test result.

### Gaps

- The current resting public data do not include an independent acquisition site, non-simultaneous EEG/fMRI, or a clinical population, so the present split cannot establish site-, session-, disease-, or asynchronous generalization. A primary neuroimaging simulation study found external-validation power depends on both training and test sample sizes, which also means NATVIEW’s 22 people should be treated as useful external evidence but not high-powered proof for small effects. [NeuroBOLT dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/NeurdyLab/NeuroBOLT); [Rosenblatt et al. (2024)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01931-7)
- It is not clear from the staged data whether motion parameters or physiological traces are available alongside all 29 scans. The loader uses the seven ROI targets, but the current data description does not establish a complete motion/respiration nuisance table; inventory the actual files before claiming nuisance-adjusted performance. [Repository data instructions](https://github.com/soupeeli/NeuroBOLT#dataset-checkpoints); [dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/NeurdyLab/NeuroBOLT)

## Which experiments are feasible with the current 29 scans, and what needs task or external data?

### Takeaway

The current data can support a disciplined set of lag/window, baseline, multi-ROI and connectivity experiments, but not broad claims about auditory tasks, non-simultaneous operation, clinical transfer or cross-site generalization. The most useful next dataset is a public second-site simultaneous EEG-fMRI cohort with both resting and structured/naturalistic conditions, evaluated without mixing participant identities across train and test.

### Cited Findings

- The NeuroBOLT release has 29 eyes-closed resting scans from 22 participants, seven repeated-scan participants, 26 EEG channels after excluding non-scalp channels, and DiFuMo-64 fMRI ROI data. It is therefore well suited to a modest subject-grouped evaluation, but the independent unit for new-person claims is 22 people, not the thousands of overlapping EEG windows. [Official dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/NeurdyLab/NeuroBOLT)
- The supplied split workbook, as read from the repository, yields 13 train participants/18 scans, four validation participants/five scans and five test participants/six scans. All repeat scans remain in one partition. The single fixed split is a good first held-out-subject check but not a stable estimate across populations. [Current split workbook](../../../scan_split_example.xlsx); [README](https://github.com/soupeeli/NeuroBOLT#configure-the-model-for-your-dataset)
- NATVIEW is an official public NKI simultaneous EEG-fMRI release with 22 people, resting-state data, checkerboard/visual tasks, naturalistic video runs, eye tracking and Biopac respiratory recordings; the dataset paper and official preprocessing repository are public. Rest scans are TR=2.1 seconds and 600 seconds; the release has many 10-minute task/naturalistic runs. This offers an external site and stimulus/task-shift challenge and better physiological nuisance analysis, though montage, task, duration, and preprocessing harmonization must be handled explicitly. [NATVIEW dataset descriptor](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-023-02458-8); [official NKI dataset page](https://fcon_1000.projects.nitrc.org/indi/retro/nat_view.html); [official preprocessing repository](https://github.com/NathanKlineInstitute/NATVIEW_EEGFMRI)
- Another public simultaneous EEG-fMRI dataset includes separate outside-scanner, scanner-on, and scanner-off recordings and a ten-minute eyes-closed simultaneous rest run, with the release organized in BIDS. It may help test scanner artifact sensitivity and preprocessing robustness, but the participant count is modest and its acquisition details differ from NeuroBOLT. [Dataset paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10585622/); [official data repository](https://doi.org/10.17632/crhybxpdy6.2)
- A 2026 MICCAI paper reports a pooled 115-subject, 831-session set of paired EEG-fMRI from six sources including NATVIEW, oddball, NODDI, rest/sleep, eyes open/closed and other simultaneous recordings. Its target is functional connectivity and it incorporates unpaired EEG datasets and a frozen fMRI teacher; its size makes it a promising route to cross-paradigm representation learning if the source data/licensing and preprocessing can be assembled, but it is not an off-the-shelf synchronized frame-level target for this repo. [MICCAI 2026 paper](https://papers.miccai.org/miccai-2026/0130-Paper0924.html)
- The public NeuroBOLT dataset card says task-condition data are available by contacting the authors rather than included in the public resting release. Thus the local M3 array can support resting reanalysis only; the paper’s task claims need access to the separate task data. [Official dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/NeurdyLab/NeuroBOLT); [current runner](../../../scripts/reproduce_rest_m3.sbatch)

### Inferences

- **Current-data queue:** first establish the reproduction score with a clean validation-selected checkpoint; then run the lag/window sweep and baseline ladder; next compare seven-ROI vs 64-ROI multi-task output; finally compare pointwise vs temporal/FC auxiliary losses. Keep all changes on the same grouped split and report subject-level uncertainty. These should be small model changes given just 13 training people.
- **External-data queue:** preprocess NATVIEW with a reproducible pipeline, map its EEG montage to compatible channels or use channel-name-aware input masking, extract the same DiFuMo-64 ROI signals, and freeze all decisions using only NeuroBOLT training/validation people. Report zero-shot external performance first, then separately report limited calibration/fine-tuning on a prespecified portion of NATVIEW subjects. Use its eye tracking/respiration data to determine whether gaze, vigilance or physiology explains prediction gains.
- **Task-generalization question:** train on rest and evaluate on NATVIEW visual/movie tasks or obtain the NeuroBOLT authors’ auditory-task release; separately assess task-conditioned and held-out-task models. A strong task-to-rest score does not automatically show rest-to-task transfer, so define direction of transfer. Use stimulus regressors only as nuisance/comparator inputs, never as hidden test information.
- **Site generalization:** with a single local site, no method can validly establish it. Treat NATVIEW as an external cohort rather than merging it randomly into the training pool; when a third paired dataset is found, test leave-one-site-out or train-one-site/test-another-site before claiming transfer.
- **Interpretation boundary:** the current release is short, eyes-closed resting data and the test set has only five people. It can show that a model predicts held-out people within this acquisition and preprocessing context; it cannot show clinical utility, absence of physiological confounding, stable across-session prediction, an EEG-only wearable reconstruction in deployment, or transfer to another scanner/site.

### Gaps

- No official code link for UnEBOLT was found on its PMLR paper page, and the released CATD/HTAN details do not establish an easy drop-in implementation. Check official supplementary material or author repositories before committing substantial porting work. [UnEBOLT](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v315/li26g.html); [CATD](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2025.3550206); [HTAN](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpe.70860)
- The actual M3-staged copy was not inspected in this research assignment, and no HPC interaction was performed. Confirm file availability, exact metadata and physiological/motion confounds locally before designing an external or nuisance-control run. [M3 runner](../../../scripts/reproduce_rest_m3.sbatch)
