# EEG Representation Learning for NeuroBOLT

## Which EEG foundation and self-supervised methods are plausible upgrades beyond LaBraM-base?

### Takeaway

The best first transfer candidate is REVE (NeurIPS 2025): its public base encoder accepts 200 Hz EEG and explicit electrode coordinates, and its design targets arbitrary montage and duration. CBraMod (ICLR 2025) and TFM-Tokenizer (ICLR 2026) are strong, public alternatives for testing factorized spatiotemporal attention and learned time-frequency tokens. Published comparisons do not establish that any one backbone will beat LaBraM for EEG-to-fMRI regression; most headline results are on different classification tasks and protocols.

### Cited Findings

- LaBraM is the current, relevant foundation baseline: its ICLR 2024 paper describes vector-quantized neural spectrum prediction followed by masked neural-code prediction, with pretraining on more than 2,500 hours from roughly 20 EEG datasets. Its official implementation publishes the base checkpoint and expects channel order metadata when adapting to another dataset. [ICLR 2024 paper](https://openreview.net/forum?id=QzTpTRVtrP); [official LaBraM repository](https://github.com/935963004/LaBraM)
- BIOT (NeurIPS 2023) was designed for heterogeneous biosignals with mismatched channel sets, variable lengths, and missing values. It tokenizes channels separately into fixed-length segments, adds channel and relative-position embeddings, and reports cross-data results across EEG, ECG, and wearable sensor datasets. [NeurIPS 2023 paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/f6b30f3e2dd9cb53bbf2024402d02295-Abstract-Conference.html); [official code and pretrained models](https://github.com/ycq091044/BIOT)
- EEGPT (NeurIPS 2024) is a 10-million-parameter model that separates spatial and temporal processing and uses dual self-supervision with spatiotemporal representation alignment rather than relying only on raw-waveform reconstruction. The paper reports strong linear-probe results on its evaluated tasks; the official repository provides an EEG-large checkpoint trained on 58 channels, 256 Hz, and four-second windows. [NeurIPS 2024 paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/4540d267eeec4e5dbd9dae9448f0b739-Abstract-Conference.html); [official repository/checkpoint instructions](https://github.com/BINE022/EEGPT)
- CBraMod (ICLR 2025) uses parallel spatial and temporal attention streams and asymmetric conditional positional encoding; its paper reports evaluations on up to 10 downstream tasks and 12 public datasets. The official implementation provides pretrained weights and accepts input shaped as batch × channels × patches × samples; its example uses 200-sample patches. [ICLR 2025 paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/bbbd6d915cb90be21c1254a82d45cedd-Abstract-Conference.html); [official repository](https://github.com/wjq-learning/CBraMod)
- REVE (NeurIPS 2025) was pretrained with masked autoencoding on more than 60,000 hours from 92 datasets and 25,000 subjects. Its 4D Fourier positional encoding incorporates electrode x/y/z coordinates and time, permitting arbitrary electrode arrangements and sequence lengths. Its paper reports an average 2.5 percentage-point balanced-accuracy gain over prior foundation models across 10 tasks and up to 17% gains in linear probing; those results are still benchmarks on tasks other than EEG-to-fMRI regression. [NeurIPS 2025 paper and results](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/20a917f77773ac0fa8bea2bdd6606b66-Paper-Conference.pdf); [official REVE repository and downstream recipes](https://github.com/elouayas/reve_eeg)
- CSBrain (NeurIPS 2025) explicitly models cross-scale spatiotemporal structure with localized temporal/anatomical tokens and structured sparse attention. The paper reports results across 11 tasks and 16 datasets. Its official code and weights are released, though its anatomical-region configuration makes it a heavier integration than a drop-in encoder. [NeurIPS 2025 paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/7e199ad8ae40eb19b2980f61f659cb07-Abstract-Conference.html); [official code and checkpoint instructions](https://github.com/yuchen2199/CSBrain)
- Uni-NTFM (ICLR 2026) jointly encodes time-domain transients and frequency-domain rhythms, uses topological embeddings for montage variation, and applies mixture-of-experts routing. It reports 28,000 hours of pretraining and 1.9 billion parameters in its largest variant; therefore, it is a useful design reference but not a sensible first model to port to the current M3 runs. The proceedings page links its code, but I did not verify a usable released checkpoint. [ICLR 2026 paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/b16e55ac49f93d33a4b87747d5121b64-Abstract-Conference.html)
- Broad 2026 benchmark conclusions are protocol-dependent. The ICLR 2026 comparison reports that fine-tuned foundation models do well in data-rich population settings, but often do not significantly beat compact or classical decoders in data-scarce settings; it also finds consistently weak linear probes and no clear model-size scaling law. The ICML 2026 EEG-FM-Bench instead reports that multi-task learning often regularizes data-scarce tasks, while emphasizing that objective alignment and adaptation compatibility matter more than scale alone. [ICLR 2026 comparison](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/f0f39b7686634fc81ca0112566b2c05f-Abstract-Conference.html); [ICML 2026 EEG-FM-Bench](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/xiong26f.html)
- A September 2026 EEG-Arena preprint reports that channel-flexible models outperform channel-constrained models across most tested channel configurations and that increasing pretraining data helps under its fixed-architecture experiments. This is not peer-reviewed yet, and its conclusions differ in emphasis from the ICLR 2026 benchmark above; treat it as evidence that matched evaluation design is still an open issue. [EEG-Arena, arXiv preprint submitted 26 September 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32743)

### Inferences

- **Priority 1: REVE-base transfer.** The local preprocessing/model interface already presents 200 Hz, 16-second, multichannel EEG-like inputs; REVE's public model card accepts 200 Hz tensors and electrode positions. A small adapter can convert each current sample to `[batch, channels, time]`, obtain positions from MNE standard montage metadata, pool REVE's channel/time tokens, and feed the existing ROI regression head. Compare frozen features, last-block/partial fine-tuning, and full fine-tuning under identical splits and seeds. [local model input contract](https://github.com/seamus-programming-stuff/NeuroBOLT/blob/main/models/model.py); [REVE model card and usage example](https://github.com/elouayas/reve_eeg/blob/main/hf/reve-base/README.md)
- **Priority 2: CBraMod transfer.** It matches the 200 Hz, 1-second patch scale used by LaBraM and can be evaluated with the existing 16-second windows, but requires an adapter that handles its channel × segment × sample tensor and returns pooled regression features. The model's learned patch and spectral projections make this a meaningful architecture comparison without rebuilding pretraining. Confirm the electrode mapping and channel-order assumptions before comparing.
- **Priority 3: TFM-Tokenizer as a front end.** Its pretrained tokenizer/encoder and MIT-licensed code/weights are public. Run tokenizer outputs per channel, preserve channel/time axes, then pool or fuse tokens into the existing regression head. The ICLR 2026 paper reports up to 11% Cohen's-kappa improvement over strong baselines and 14% improvement on an ear-EEG sleep task, which supports testing the representation but does not predict an fMRI-regression gain. [ICLR 2026 paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/3dc85735f6e2fcf093e67b134fa00d21-Abstract-Conference.html); [official code and public pretrained checkpoints](https://github.com/Jathurshan0330/TFM-Tokenizer)
- Keep the LaBraM baseline as the control. The available cohort's 29 scans and resting-state-only coverage are very different from the large heterogeneous pretraining corpora and classification benchmarks. The research question should be “does this representation improve held-out-subject fMRI prediction here?” rather than extrapolating a published EEG classification rank.

### Gaps

- I found no peer-reviewed head-to-head evaluation of LaBraM, REVE, CBraMod, or EEGPT on this NeuroBOLT EEG-to-fMRI regression setup. Public papers' gains should be treated as motivation for local tests, not a predicted improvement.
- The currently installed data are resting-state only. They cannot support claims about transfer to the active auditory task or other cognitive tasks without acquiring and preprocessing those scans.
- Before REVE/CBraMod/CSBrain adapters are implemented, verify exact channel names, reference, MNE coordinate coverage, and sampling rate from the current EEG metadata. The model-side interface appears aligned to 200 Hz, but exact montage compatibility depends on that metadata.

## How do recent methods handle montage shifts, subject shifts, and limited-label adaptation?

### Takeaway

Geometry-aware channel encoding is the cleanest structural upgrade: replace ordinal channel IDs with electrode coordinates, and evaluate subject-held-out rather than random-window generalization. For the small downstream cohort, compare a frozen encoder/ridge head, LoRA or last-block tuning, and full fine-tuning. Do not assume unsupervised test-time adaptation helps: a 2026 EEG-specific benchmark found inconsistent gains and frequent regressions from gradient-based TTA.

### Cited Findings

- NeuroBOLT currently adds learned absolute channel-index and time-index embeddings to the spatiotemporal tokens. Its spectral encoder also owns an `nn.Embedding(n_channels, 200)` channel-token table (default 26 channels), so both paths encode channel identity through learned indices rather than explicit scalp coordinates. [NeuroBOLT spatiotemporal model](https://github.com/seamus-programming-stuff/NeuroBOLT/blob/main/models/model.py); [NeuroBOLT multiscale spectral model](https://github.com/seamus-programming-stuff/NeuroBOLT/blob/main/models/model_multiscale.py)
- REVE's coordinate-based 4D positional encoding is explicitly designed to work across electrode configurations and arbitrary sequence lengths. The public HF example takes electrode names, looks up xyz positions, and applies the checkpoint to 200 Hz EEG. Access to the pretrained checkpoint is gated by a responsible-use agreement, including no redistribution of weights or attempts to identify subjects; the official code is public. [NeurIPS 2025 REVE paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/20a917f77773ac0fa8bea2bdd6606b66-Paper-Conference.pdf); [REVE-base model card](https://github.com/elouayas/reve_eeg/blob/main/hf/reve-base/README.md)
- CBraMod uses asymmetric conditional positional encoding and parallel spatial/temporal attention rather than LaBraM's single flattened spatiotemporal attention sequence. Its model implementation takes a variable `ch_num` dimension, but the public checkpoint's training/data setup uses standard 10–20 EEG and local depthwise position convolutions; matching the channel layout/order still matters. [ICLR 2025 CBraMod paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/bbbd6d915cb90be21c1254a82d45cedd-Abstract-Conference.html); [official model source](https://github.com/wjq-learning/CBraMod/blob/main/models/cbramod.py)
- BIOT handles mismatched channels, variable length, and missing channels through channel-wise tokens and channel embeddings. This gives it flexibility, though the public official repository distributes different pretrained checkpoints for channel configurations/data mixtures; inspect the specific checkpoint's channel map before using it as a common reference. [NeurIPS 2023 paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/f6b30f3e2dd9cb53bbf2024402d02295-Abstract-Conference.html); [official BIOT repository](https://github.com/ycq091044/BIOT)
- EEGPT's released pretrained model uses 58 channels at 256 Hz over 4 seconds. It is available, but adapting the current 26-channel/200 Hz input requires explicit resampling and montage mapping; it is a useful third-party representation baseline, not the lowest-friction transfer candidate. [official EEGPT repository](https://github.com/BINE022/EEGPT)
- The ICLR 2026 broad evaluation tested six protocols and found that foundation-model advantages shrink in data-scarce settings, frozen linear probes are weak, and model rankings vary by task. The ICML 2026 EEG-FM-Bench reports that multi-task training often helps regularize low-data EEG tasks, with some task-dependent negative transfer. [ICLR 2026 evaluation](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/f0f39b7686634fc81ca0112566b2c05f-Abstract-Conference.html); [ICML 2026 EEG-FM-Bench](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/xiong26f.html)
- An EEG-specific PEFT study (IEEE BCI Conference 2026) evaluated LaBraM on a 54-subject motor-imagery set with leave-one-subject-out splits and few-shot calibration. LoRA had lower overall accuracy than a DeepConvNet specialist, but subject-wise gains were more consistent than partial fine-tuning; the authors report over 100× fewer trainable parameters and 35% shorter epoch time versus partial fine-tuning. This is evidence to include LoRA in the comparison, not evidence that it will beat NeuroBOLT full fine-tuning. [IEEE 2026 paper](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11435102/)
- NeuroAdapt-Bench (Machine Learning for Healthcare, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 340, August 2026) tests EEG foundation models under in-distribution, out-of-distribution, and modality shifts. Existing TTA methods had inconsistent performance and often degraded results, especially gradient-based methods; optimization-free approaches were more stable. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v340/lee26a.html); [official benchmark code](https://github.com/leegabriel/NeuroAdapt-Bench)

### Inferences

- **Coordinate-aware ablation:** add an xyz/Fourier channel-position embedding to each channel-time token in place of (or alongside) the learned ordinal embedding. For NeuroBOLT's spectral branch, replace `channel_tokens[index]` with an MLP/Fourier projection of electrode coordinates. Initialize the rest from the current LaBraM-compatible checkpoint and test three variants: ordinal ID (current), coordinates only, and ID + coordinates. This can be evaluated on the existing fixed montage now; its montage robustness requires an external montage, channel-dropout, or controlled channel-subset test.
- **Subject-generalization protocol:** report subject-grouped folds or leave-one-subject-out, and keep each subject's scans entirely within one split. The existing 29-subject setup is the relevant source for a cross-subject claim; a random split across highly autocorrelated EEG windows would answer a different, easier question. Keep the existing scan-level experiment untouched while it runs, then add a separate grouped-CV experiment after the baseline result has been collected.
- **Adaptation ladder:** at fixed data/split/seed, compare (1) ridge/linear head on frozen features, (2) trainable ROI head + final Transformer block, (3) LoRA on `blocks.*.attn.qkv` and `attn.proj` (the local attention uses fused QKV), (4) trainable spectral branch + regression head, and (5) full fine-tuning. Choose LoRA rank 4/8 and a low LR as small initial search values, not as literature-backed optimum. Report trainable parameter count, GPU time, and both within-scan and held-out-subject scores.
- **Multi-ROI training:** the current workflow appears to train a separate model for each ROI (`num_roi`/`labels_roi` exist, while the data pipeline selects the requested ROI). A shared encoder with a seven-output regression head could exploit correlated targets as a low-data regularizer, motivated by EEG-FM-Bench's multi-task findings. Compare joint training with per-ROI runs and monitor per-ROI negative transfer rather than assuming joint training helps. [model construction](https://github.com/seamus-programming-stuff/NeuroBOLT/blob/main/models/model.py); [ICML 2026 EEG-FM-Bench](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/xiong26f.html)
- Avoid online gradient TTA as the first personalization experiment. If a subject-specific setting is scientifically useful, use a clearly separated calibration subset with labels and compare frozen-head, LoRA, and partial tuning. If trying unsupervised TTA, include a no-adaptation control and check each held-out subject for regressions.

### Gaps

- No channel-shift experiment is possible from a single fixed acquisition montage alone. Channel dropout/subset tests can test sensitivity, but true cross-device transfer needs another acquisition setup or public dataset; channel interpolation should be a separately specified preprocessing baseline.
- The available information does not establish exact channel coordinates or whether the current channel labels match a standard MNE montage. Check metadata before choosing coordinate positions or a cross-backbone channel mapping.
- The reported LoRA result is from motor imagery classification and few-shot labeled calibration, not resting-state fMRI prediction. The optimal target modules, rank, and adaptation depth for this regression objective remain empirical questions.

## Which time-frequency or multiscale changes have evidence, and what should be ablated in this code?

### Takeaway

NeuroBOLT already has an explicit multiscale STFT stream, so the most informative extensions are controlled changes to scale fusion, frequency tokenization, and spatiotemporal attention—not simply adding a second spectral branch. Test a learnable scale gate, a TFM-Tokenizer front end, and a factorized channel/time encoder. For new self-supervised pretraining, the cutting-edge direction is to predict structured latent targets rather than noisy raw EEG, but this should be a separate post-reproduction project using training-subject EEG only.

### Cited Findings

- NeuroBOLT's current `MSSEncoder` computes STFT magnitudes at `n_fft` 100, 200, 400, and 800 samples by default, with `hop_length=n_fft` and `center=False`; it projects and sums the four scales, adds a learned per-channel token, then applies a linear-attention Transformer. The spatial-temporal encoder separately maps channel/time patches through a Transformer and sums its representation with the spectral stream. [NeuroBOLT model implementation](https://github.com/seamus-programming-stuff/NeuroBOLT/blob/main/models/model.py); [NeuroBOLT multiscale implementation](https://github.com/seamus-programming-stuff/NeuroBOLT/blob/main/models/model_multiscale.py)
- CBraMod (ICLR 2025) argues that EEG's spatial and temporal dependencies differ structurally, then models them in parallel using criss-cross attention. Its patch embedding combines temporal convolution and per-patch FFT magnitude before adding a local depthwise positional convolution. [ICLR 2025 paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/bbbd6d915cb90be21c1254a82d45cedd-Abstract-Conference.html); [official implementation](https://github.com/wjq-learning/CBraMod/blob/main/models/cbramod.py)
- CSBrain (NeurIPS 2025) builds cross-scale tokens from local time windows and anatomical regions, then applies structured sparse attention to cross-window/cross-region relations; the reported evaluation spans 11 tasks and 16 datasets. This is the clearest recent empirical reason to test scale-aware regional structure, although its task results do not establish fMRI regression benefit. [NeurIPS 2025 paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/7e199ad8ae40eb19b2980f61f659cb07-Abstract-Conference.html)
- TFM-Tokenizer (ICLR 2026) learns discrete time-frequency motifs from single-channel EEG using separate spectral and temporal paths, frequency-band and time masking, and masked motif prediction. Its official release includes a pretrained tokenizer and an MTP-pretrained encoder; the paper reports gains on four EEG benchmarks and tests out-of-domain ear-EEG. [ICLR 2026 paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/3dc85735f6e2fcf093e67b134fa00d21-Abstract-Conference.html); [official repository/checkpoints](https://github.com/Jathurshan0330/TFM-Tokenizer)
- Uni-NTFM (ICLR 2026) uses a heterogeneous projection for temporal transients and frequency rhythms, topological sensor embeddings, and routed experts; its reported pretraining corpus is 28,000 hours. Its design supports multiscale/frequency-aware fusion as a research idea, but its 1.9B-parameter largest model is an impractical target for a first local experiment and I could not verify a pretrained checkpoint release. [ICLR 2026 paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/b16e55ac49f93d33a4b87747d5121b64-Abstract-Conference.html)
- EEG-JEPA (arXiv preprint, 31 July 2026) replaces direct masked waveform reconstruction with prediction of contextual latent states from an EMA teacher; its code is public. A separate arXiv preprint submitted 27 September 2026 reports a controlled comparison of 58 MAE/JEPA models that varies spatiotemporal mask geometry, with multiple settings statistically indistinguishable from its best configuration. Both are preprints as of 4 October 2026, not settled evidence for a winning objective. [EEG-JEPA preprint and code](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00114); [mask-geometry preprint and experiment set](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33487)
- EEG-FM-Bench (ICML 2026) reports that multi-task learning can regularize data-limited pretraining/downstream contexts, but may produce negative transfer; the authors also identify conflicts between reconstruction pretraining objectives and downstream tasks. This supports measuring objective/task alignment and per-target effects, not adding auxiliary losses without ablation. [ICML 2026 paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/xiong26f.html)

### Inferences

- **Smallest direct code change — learned scale fusion.** Replace `torch.sum(torch.stack(channel_spec_emb_list), dim=0)` with four learned scale weights (global softmax weights first; input-conditioned gates second). Ablate: current equal sum; learned global weights; per-window gates; each scale alone; and the full set. Keep the temporal path and data split fixed. This specifically tests whether short/transient vs long/rhythmic windows help map to fMRI BOLD without changing the pretrained temporal backbone.
- **Second change — overlap at coarse scales.** Current STFT uses non-overlapping windows (`hop=n_fft`); at `n_fft=800` a 16-second sample yields only four windows. Try overlap (e.g. hop `n_fft // 2`) as an isolated temporal-resolution ablation and log cost/VRAM. Compare fixed patch count carefully, since more tokens change both resolution and compute.
- **Third change — TFM front end.** Preserve raw input and preprocessing, make STFT with the released helper, encode individual channels through pretrained TFM tokenizer, then fuse the token sequence with either the current regression head or a small attention pool. Ablate tokenizer frozen vs fine-tuned, raw branch vs motif-token branch, and spectral-only vs time-frequency tokenizer. Do not compare this with different split policies.
- **Fourth change — factorized/fusion structure.** Use channel × time tensor tokens and alternating channel-attention/time-attention blocks (CBraMod-like), or group channels by anatomical region and use sparse cross-region attention (CSBrain-like). This tests whether local spatial organization improves on the existing flattened single attention stream. For a modest first trial, reuse the same hidden size and 12 temporal blocks and add only two factorized blocks to keep parameters/computation controlled.
- **Self-supervised follow-up:** after the current full resting-state reproduction finishes, pretrain an encoder on unlabeled EEG from training subjects only. Compare raw masked signal reconstruction (MAE-like) with masked frequency-magnitude reconstruction and latent-target prediction (EEG-JEPA-like), using identical backbone/data/mask geometry. Ensure held-out subject EEG is not used in pretraining if reporting subject-generalization; an explicitly transductive experiment should be labeled separately. The 29 scans are not comparable in scale to the corpora used for the large FMs, so keep this as a local inductive-bias experiment rather than a foundation-model scaling claim.
- **Recommended ablation sequence:** preserve the running paper reproduction untouched; after results are gathered, establish a post-run branch/control, then test (1) frozen REVE + identical regression head, (2) REVE partial/LoRA/full tuning, (3) current branch with scale-gate ablations, (4) CBraMod or TFM tokens, and (5) multi-ROI shared-head or self-supervised objective. Use multiple seeds and subject-grouped evaluation; report mean/SD and per-subject/per-ROI metrics. Include a compact specialist baseline trained from scratch because the 2026 benchmark literature finds these remain competitive in small-data settings.

### Gaps

- Recent time-frequency papers mostly report classification or seizure/sleep tasks, not continuous fMRI prediction. No paper-level evidence identifies the best spectral front end for this specific target.
- The current `MSSEncoder` may depend on assumptions about sample length and fixed scale projections. Verify tensor shapes for overlapping STFT before implementing it; the existing scale embeddings are initialized for the current 16-second/3200-sample geometry.
- EEG-JEPA and the September 2026 masking-geometry comparison are newly posted preprints. Their preliminary results justify a small controlled test, not replacing the published reproduction objective or claiming state of the art.
- The exact downstream fMRI target alignment (same-time vs lagged BOLD and whether the current y is preprocessed per scan or across scans) should be preserved in each architecture ablation; changing it at the same time would confound interpretation.
