# Clinical endpoints and sample sizes for EEG-derived functional MRI

## 1. What has actually been clinically validated, and what is the indication?

### Takeaway

The strongest verified regulatory precedent is a narrow fMRI-informed EEG neurofeedback intervention, Prism for adjunctive PTSD treatment. It is not authorization to synthesize diagnostic functional images. This search identified no clinically validated, regulator-authorized EEG-only replacement for patient-specific diagnostic/presurgical fMRI; that is a bounded search finding, not proof that none exists anywhere. [FDA Prism record and decision, 17 March 2023](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpmn/pmn.cfm?ID=K222101); [cleared indications and clinical summary](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K222101.pdf).

### Cited Findings

- **Scope and date:** evidence checked for an assessment current to **21 October 2026, Australia/Sydney**. Page-specific publication/update dates take precedence over search-engine labels such as “published yesterday.” The FDA AI lifecycle page still labels its January 2025 document **draft, not for implementation**; the TGA AI page gives an update date of 5 February 2026. [FDA lifecycle guidance status](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/artificial-intelligence-enabled-device-software-functions-lifecycle-management-and-marketing); [TGA AI regulation](https://www.tga.gov.au/products/medical-devices/software-and-artificial-intelligence-ai/manufacturing/artificial-intelligence-ai-and-medical-device-software).
- **Prism: official clearance, not diagnostic imaging approval.** K222101 is a Class II biofeedback device, cleared by substantial equivalence on 17 March 2023. Its indication is EEG biofeedback for relaxation/stress reduction and adjunctive treatment of PTSD symptoms under a health professional, alongside other interventions. The summary explicitly excludes diagnostic conclusions/classification/clinical interpretation. The supporting study was prospective, single-arm, open-label and unblinded: five sites, four Israeli and one US; 101 screened, 79 in the full analysis set, 66 efficacy, 63 per-protocol. The endpoint was a ≥6-point CAPS-5 reduction at three months; 66.67% of the efficacy set responded. This is a clinical outcome study, not a held-out synthesis benchmark. [FDA decision and summary, pp. 3–8](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K222101.pdf).
- **Fruchter et al., 2024: peer-reviewed clinical study.** The Psychiatry Research article describes the multinational prospective Amyg-EFP neurofeedback study; its uncontrolled design cannot isolate benefit from concurrent care, expectancy, natural history, or repeated contact. Do not count the publication and the FDA supporting study as independent replications: the same trial identifier NCT04891614 and matching cohort structure indicate the same underlying trial. The registry protocol calls it a prospective single-arm open-label pilot. [Published article DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychres.2023.115711); [full article, accessible PDF copy](https://starlegacyfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/Fruchter-2024-Amygdala-derived-EEG-fMRI-pattern-neurofeedback-for-the-treatment-of-PTSD.pdf); [registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04891614); [registered protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/14/NCT04891614/Prot_SAP_000.pdf).
- **Related controlled clinical work exists.** Fruchtman-Steinbok et al., 2021 randomized 59 adults with PTSD to trauma-script feedback, neutral feedback, or no neurofeedback, with 15 sessions and actual fMRI neurofeedback to assess transfer. Its authors describe proof of concept and unresolved exposure/placebo contributions. This is EEG-only delivery of an amygdala-related feedback signal plus separate MRI assessment, not MRI-free whole-brain validation. The abstract does not establish a multisite independent imaging test. [Peer-reviewed full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8551212/).
- **NeuroBOLT: peer-reviewed conference research, not clinical device validation.** The arXiv record reports NeurIPS 2024 acceptance. The inspected v2 methods use 29 resting scans from 22 healthy people, plus 16 auditory-task scans from 10 people at another site. Inputs are simultaneous in-scanner EEG; outputs are separately modeled ROI/global BOLD time courses, not diagnostic volumes or validated task maps. Resting train/tuning/test split: 18/5/6 scans, grouping repeated people; task: 9/3/4 scans. Same-scan experiments train on 80%, tune on 10%, test on 10%, with 20-second gaps. Pearson R is temporal prediction-versus-measurement correlation; reported averages combine selected targets and scan-level results, not clinical sensitivity. Test-set unique-person counts and equal-person/Fisher weighting are not established here. [Versioned methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05341v2); [publication/version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05341).
- **NT-ViT: ECCV 2024 workshop research, arXiv full text inspected.** NODDI: 17 originally, 15 analyzed people, 4,500 paired samples; oddball: 17 people, 17,340 paired samples. These are simultaneous scanner recordings from two datasets, not thousands of independent people. Outputs are fMRI-volume predictions; fixed tests contain two NODDI or four oddball people, with additional leave-one-subject-out evaluation. The two datasets are evaluated separately, so this is not a demonstrated unseen-site transfer design. Main metrics are RMSE, SSIM and PSNR, not R or presurgical accuracy. The abstract explicitly disclaims replacement of actual fMRI. Clinical cohort/site counts are not provided as a clinical trial. [Full methods and results](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11836v1); [workshop/publication record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11836).
- **CATD: peer-reviewed IEEE TMI 2025 research, with accessible author manuscript.** Its manuscript reports a 10-person motor-imagery dataset, a NODDI synthesis test on two named held-out people, and an eight-person Parkinson EEG dataset. The Parkinson demonstration generates BOLD maps from EEG without paired patient fMRI, then performs disease classification. Therefore its high classifier accuracy and illustrated “abnormal” regions do not verify those patients' regional BOLD measurements. Patient-level classification partitioning, independence of windows and external clinical reference localization are not sufficiently established by the inspected passages; do not turn classification accuracy into patient-level imaging sensitivity. The manuscript’s strong clinical interpretations exceed what this evidence alone establishes. [Author manuscript, especially III-A and III-E](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00777); [journal record](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/10922738).
- **Current-to-2026 check:** UniEFS, *Mind the State*, is an ICML 2026 proceedings paper proposing temporally resolved whole-brain synthesis with a pretrained spatial decoder and metadata prompts, including potential zero-shot transfer to clinical populations. The proceedings abstract was accessible; its linked full PDF did not resolve in this research session. Participant counts, pretraining overlap, clinical subgroups, held-out sites, R aggregation, and scanner/outside-scanner transfer therefore remain unverified here. Do not describe it as a clinical trial or assume only 2024 methods exist. [Original proceedings and abstract](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/li26gp.html).
- **Another 2026 caution:** Zhang et al., HTAN, is a peer-reviewed July 2026 journal article, but only its abstract was accessible. It reports group mean scan-wise Pearson correlation 0.82±0.02 and explicitly describes anatomical-space renderings as visualizations of ROI predictions, not direct voxel reconstruction. Number of people/sites, split independence, uncertainty definition and preprocessing could not be verified; do not compare this R numerically with the internal results or interpret it as clinically validated performance. [Original publisher abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/cpe.70860).
- **Simultaneous EEG-informed fMRI is a different comparator.** Koupparis et al., 2021 evaluated 106 surgeries in 84 epilepsy patients, relating actual EEG-fMRI responses to resection and ≥1-year outcomes. It is not 106 independent patients and not EEG-only synthesis. An et al., 2013 studied 35 surgical patients selected for significant actual EEG-fMRI responses. These studies provide clinical hypotheses/endpoints, not an authorization to replace the scanner. [2021 original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34400584/); [2013 peer-reviewed full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4498907/).

### Inferences

- **Lock a separate indication before recommending data or endpoints.** Proposed A: “alert the scanner technologist to sustained loss of wakefulness during a specified adult resting acquisition, prompting review.” Proposed B: “provide one specified amygdala-related EEG feedback signal as an adjunct for a specified patient population.” Proposed C1: “substitute for one named functional-MRI language-lateralization assessment in specified epilepsy candidates.” Proposed C2: “localize a putative epileptogenic region to influence invasive monitoring or surgery.” C1 and C2 require distinct evidence. These are proposed indications, not existing approvals or equivalent claims. Their evidence must follow intended use. [IMDRF clinical evaluation](https://www.imdrf.org/sites/default/files/docs/imdrf/final/technical/imdrf-tech-170921-samd-n41-clinical-evaluation_1.pdf).
- “EEG-derived fMRI” can mean a scalar/time course, an atlas-painted signal, static functional connectivity, task activation maps, or temporally resolved voxel volumes. Success in one output does not validate the others. A model consuming anatomical MRI, patient-specific paired calibration, task timing, or precomputed fMRI targets has a different acquisition claim from EEG-only inference. Inspect each input at deployment and training/test overlap before using “MRI-free.” This is an inference from the heterogeneous designs above, not a new empirical comparison. [NeuroBOLT methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05341v2); [NT-ViT methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11836v1); [HTAN abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/cpe.70860).
- **Functional-MRI substitution is not anatomical-MRI substitution.** EEG records electrophysiological activity; learned spatial priors cannot establish an individual’s lesion anatomy. Even a successful functional surrogate would not supply anatomical MRI for lesion detection, structural characterization, registration or surgical planning. Retain necessary anatomical imaging. This recommendation follows the measurement/output distinction and the task-specific scope of clinical fMRI guidance; it is not a claim that functional MRI itself is an anatomical reference standard. [NeuroBOLT physiological task formulation](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05341v2); [AAN presurgical functional-MRI guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5272968/).

### Gaps

- Search coverage: official TGA/FDA/IMDRF pages; ClinicalTrials.gov-indexed searches for EEG, synthetic fMRI, replacement and Prism; primary publisher/arXiv/proceedings searches through the cutoff. No verified EEG-only diagnostic/presurgical fMRI-replacement authorization or prospective replacement-utility trial was identified. This was not an exhaustive search of every registry, the full ARTG, or all device trade names; do not write “there are no devices/trials.” Australian marketing status of Prism was not verified.
- UniEFS full methods and HTAN full text require further access before quantitative comparison. CATD’s manuscript is not a substitute for auditing all journal supplements and subject IDs. E2fNet and infant static-FC work surfaced but were not fully audited; neither is used here as clinical validation evidence. Direct PMC/BMJ retrieval intermittently failed; publisher-indexed passages and accessible full copies were used where indicated. No claims depend on inaccessible numerical tables.
- **Internal starting point supplied by the user; not peer reviewed, not independently audited, no public source URL:** 22 people/29 VU resting scans; full ridge seven-target R .381 versus shared 24 s NeuroBOLT .336 in subject-grouped CV. Removing global signal changes 64-region mean R .343 to .144. Frozen VU ridge on 28 ds006040 people gives eyes-closed R .227 versus .228 trained within site, and eyes-open .140. These close values do not demonstrate equivalence. A global-BOLD arousal-monitor prototype gives streaming-replay r .263, not a prospective live scanner trial. Exact averaging/weighting and uncertainty for these internal R statistics were not supplied. Do not infer a biological or model ceiling from the small VU learning curve. These statements are intentionally recorded as assignment context, not externally verified findings.

## 2. Which clinical endpoints and error trade-offs matter, and why is R insufficient?

### Takeaway

Clinical value depends on the action taken for an individual and the consequences of error. Time-course correlation can support technical characterization, but cannot establish correct alerting, useful neurofeedback, localization, safe surgical decisions, or patient benefit. FDA diagnostic guidance distinguishes accuracy against a suitable reference from mere agreement with a comparator. [FDA diagnostic-test reporting guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/statistical-guidance-reporting-results-studies-evaluating-diagnostic-tests-guidance-industry-and-fda).

### Cited Findings

- TRIPOD+AI, 2024, is a 27-item **reporting guideline** for prediction models using regression or machine learning. It distinguishes discrimination, calibration and clinical utility, and requires transparent reporting of development and evaluation. It does not prescribe a universal subject count or R cutoff. [Original BMJ statement](https://www.bmj.com/content/385/bmj-2023-078378).
- The AAN’s 2017 guideline treats actual clinical fMRI as indication-specific: language lateralization, memory lateralization and prediction of postoperative memory decline have different evidence levels. Tasks, processing, patient cooperation and medications affect results. It does not validate synthetic fMRI, and its recommendations must not be generalized to every epilepsy subtype or to all presurgical decisions. [Original clinical guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5272968/).
- Physiological shared variance is plausible and clinically relevant: Bolt et al., 2025 report low-frequency global-fMRI covariation with EEG and multiple peripheral autonomic signals across independent samples and acquisition sequences. This is a mechanistic study, not a replacement trial; detailed cohort counts were not recoverable from the abstract, so no cohort-size claim is made. [Peer-reviewed original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40335772/).
- Mehta et al., arXiv v2, is treated here as a **preprint**, not assumed peer-reviewed. It analyzes subject-specific somatomotor BOLD prediction with same-person cross-session and within-session designs, with results tabulated over 15 people. Same-session performance was more optimistic. This is not unseen-person or unseen-site validation, and the authors’ description of leakage should not be read as proof that every same-session split is invalid. [Full preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10752v2); [version/publication record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10752).

### Inferences

All targets below are **proposed planning choices for the stated indication**, requiring clinician/patient input and pilot measurement. They are not standards or regulatory acceptance criteria. The clinical validation rationale is IMDRF’s intended-use framework. [IMDRF N41](https://www.imdrf.org/sites/default/files/docs/imdrf/final/technical/imdrf-tech-170921-samd-n41-clinical-evaluation_1.pdf).

| Locked application | Reference and useful endpoints | Error trade-off and point of use |
|---|---|---|
| A1: sustained loss-of-wakefulness advisory during a specified adult resting scan | Prespecified sustained episode definition; blinded independent sleep/vigilance scoring, with eye tracking/behavioral measures as appropriate. Episode sensitivity, false alarms per scan/hour, time to detection, positive predictive value, calibrated alert probability, usable monitoring coverage. For a claim about scan quality, add independent blinded scan-quality adjudication. | Missing poor acquisition loses usable data; unnecessary alarms interrupt useful scans or cause repeat imaging. Arousal is not synonymous with motion, and sleep may be desired in some protocols. Show “review wakefulness/signal quality,” with the technologist deciding what to do. |
| A2: improvement in scan acquisition | Prospectively compare usable planned acquisitions, repeat scans, acquisition time, patient tolerance and staff burden under an alert policy versus usual practice. Report failures and adverse workflow effects. | A good physiological predictor can still worsen acquisition if it distracts staff. Separate silent prospective evaluation from an interventional alert trial. |
| B1: one named ROI/network signal surrogate | Paired real fMRI for technical validation; between-day repeatability, signed bias, amplitude/slope calibration, MAE/RMSE, limits of agreement, temporal lag, response sign and target engagement. Prespecify whether the target includes or excludes global BOLD. | Rewarding artifacts, global arousal, muscle tension or breathing instead of the intended target can invalidate feedback. Include physiological comparators and task-only baselines. Show a labeled surrogate signal and uncertainty, not an anatomical diagnosis. |
| B2: therapeutic neurofeedback for one disease | Target engagement is mechanistic support; clinical benefit requires validated symptom/function endpoints, adverse effects, durability and an appropriate active/sham/usual-care comparator. Blind assessors; assess expectancy and adherence. | A feedback signal may help without being an accurate regional BOLD measurement. Conversely, high correlation does not prove a clinically useful treatment. Claim the demonstrated intervention benefit and mechanism at the supported level. |
| C1: replace one functional assessment, such as specified language lateralization | Patient-level wrong-side rate; agreement with actual fMRI; where clinically appropriate, independent mapping and postoperative language/memory outcome. For local maps: distance to clinically important cortex, missed eloquent sites, spurious activations, and uncertainty in registration. | Wrong localization can cause irreversible deficits. Real fMRI is an imperfect comparator; agreement alone is not diagnostic truth. Retain anatomical MRI and standard escalation when results are discordant or uninformative. |
| C2: epilepsy localization influencing invasive monitoring/resection | Localization against appropriate clinical evidence, including intracranial EEG where available; multidisciplinary adjudication blinded to synthesis; seizure freedom/recurrence and new deficits after ≥1-year follow-up as prespecified outcomes. Include patients not proceeding to surgery. | Healthy controls cannot establish specificity among difficult epilepsy mimics, bilateral/multifocal cases or abnormal anatomy. Resection/outcome is an imperfect, selected reference, not a simple gold standard. Additional actual EEG-fMRI association does not establish replacement utility. |
| C3: patient-specific regional/whole-brain functional reconstruction | Validate the named downstream decisions as well as regional time courses, maps and connectivity. Report region-specific errors, worst cases, abnormality preservation, and clinically relevant strata. A static-FC result does not validate moment-to-moment volumes or task maps. | Spatial averaging can hide an error at one critical region; visually plausible volumes can convey unjustified certainty. A full-brain display is not itself a justified clinical indication. |

**Why correlation alone fails — mathematical and design reasoning:**

1. If predicted signal is `10 × true signal + 100`, Pearson r is 1 for a nonconstant true signal, despite severe scale/offset error. Therefore r does not establish amplitude calibration or threshold accuracy. Probability calibration is a separate question again. TRIPOD+AI treats calibration separately from discrimination. [TRIPOD+AI](https://www.bmj.com/content/385/bmj-2023-078378).
2. Temporal r within one ROI/scan, spatial r across voxels, r between connectivity edges, and a correlation of group mean responses are different estimands. Report the definition, timescale, lag selection, filtering, normalization, sign, within-scan duration and aggregation. State whether averaging is arithmetic or Fisher-z and whether people or scans receive equal weight. Avoid comparing values across different output types. [NeuroBOLT methods and metrics](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05341v2); [HTAN’s scan-wise definition](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/cpe.70860).
3. A shared global/arousal component can support regional correlations without sufficient evidence of region-specific information. Removal of that component can also remove meaningful physiology: performance with and without global signal answers different questions. The internal decrease is a reason to investigate, not proof that all remaining signal is artifact. [Bolt et al., physiological evidence](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40335772/).
4. Overlapping EEG windows, autocorrelated BOLD, repeated sessions and shared task blocks inflate apparent information if treated as independent. Training/testing on the same person is not unseen-person generalization; person grouping alone is not unseen-site generalization. Evaluate full recordings and cluster uncertainty by person, with sites represented explicitly. [Mehta et al., preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10752v2).
5. Scanner EEG may retain gradient, ballistocardiographic, motion and environment-specific features. Training on simultaneous scanner data does not demonstrate outside-scanner performance. Validate the intended cap, reference, montage, acquisition hardware, preprocessing latency and environment; use separate-session scanner/outside-scanner studies where outside-scanner use is claimed. [NeuroBOLT scanner acquisition and artifact methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05341v2).
6. Prespecify a quality gate and abstention rule. Report coverage, reasons for failure, performance conditional on output, and outcomes for everyone enrolled. Abstentions are not automatically errors, but dropping them can make a low-coverage device look safe. Report the complete clinical pathway including the rescue test and burden it creates. [FDA diagnostic guidance on equivocal results](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/statistical-guidance-reporting-results-studies-evaluating-diagnostic-tests-guidance-industry-and-fda).

### Gaps

- No universal minimum r for any of these locked indications was identified. Neither r .3 nor r .9 is an established general clinical acceptance threshold.
- Clinically acceptable missed-event rates, false alarms/hour, localization tolerances, minimum coverage, treatment effect and rescue policy must be elicited for a specific workflow. They cannot be recovered from the VU correlation numbers.
- Needed physiological strata include sleep/wake state, eyes open/closed, medication/sedation, disease severity, neurovascular changes, respiration/CO2, cardiovascular signals, head motion and EEG artifact. Their effects and relevant distributions for the eventual target population remain unknown.

## 3. How many independent positive/negative cases or events would precision require?

### Takeaway

Sample size follows the estimand and precision target. The arithmetic below estimates the validation denominators for a frozen decision threshold; it does not estimate the training set needed to achieve that performance, prove a lower-bound performance requirement, or establish a regulatory threshold. Buderer’s original method explicitly incorporates expected sensitivity, specificity, precision and prevalence. [Buderer, 1996, original paper abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8870764/).

### Cited Findings

- FDA recommends reporting numerator/denominator pairs and two-sided 95% intervals, with results by relevant sites and subgroups. Against an unsuitable/non-reference comparator, use agreement terminology rather than treating agreement as unbiased clinical sensitivity/specificity. Score/Wilson and exact intervals are discussed in the guidance. [FDA diagnostic guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/statistical-guidance-reporting-results-studies-evaluating-diagnostic-tests-guidance-industry-and-fda).
- External prediction-model sample-size methods consider multiple quantities, including discrimination, calibration and decision thresholds; “100 events” is not a universal validation solution. [Riley et al., 2024, original methods article](https://www.bmj.com/content/384/bmj-2023-074821).

### Inferences

**All numerical scenarios in this and the following sections are illustrative planning assumptions and calculations, not empirically established prevalence/performance or regulatory rules.** Assume a locked model/threshold, an appropriate blinded reference, representative cases within each class, independent observations, and a two-sided 95% confidence level. The usual normal approximation is:

```text
n_positive = ceil[1.96² × Se × (1 − Se) / d_Se²]
n_negative = ceil[1.96² × Sp × (1 − Sp) / d_Sp²]
N_consecutive = max[ceil(n_positive / prevalence),
                    ceil(n_negative / (1 − prevalence))]
N_recruited ≈ ceil(N_analyzable / (1 − expected_loss))
```

Here “positive” means positive by the reference, not a positive device prediction. A patient can be target-positive for an outcome without having a different underlying disease from a target-negative patient. These formulas are precision approximations motivated by the original diagnostic-design method. [Buderer](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8870764/).

| Assumed proportion to estimate | Desired approximate 95% half-width | Arithmetic before rounding | Required independent reference-class denominator |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sensitivity .90 | .05 (5 percentage points) | 3.8416 × .90 × .10 / .0025 = 138.2976 | 139 positive patients/events |
| Sensitivity .90 | .03 | 3.8416 × .90 × .10 / .0009 = 384.16 | 385 positive patients/events |
| Sensitivity .95 | .05 | 3.8416 × .95 × .05 / .0025 = 72.9904 | 73 positive patients/events |
| Sensitivity .95 | .03 | 3.8416 × .95 × .05 / .0009 = 202.7511 | 203 positive patients/events |
| Specificity .95 | .03 | Same last calculation | 203 negative patients/events |
| Specificity .95 | .02 | 3.8416 × .95 × .05 / .0004 = 456.19 | 457 negative patients/events |
| Specificity .99 | .005 | 3.8416 × .99 × .01 / .000025 = 1521.2736 | 1,522 negative patients/events |

**Finite-sample qualification:** these are not guaranteed ±d intervals around the observed estimate. At 69/73, observed sensitivity is 94.52%; a Wilson interval using z=1.96 is approximately **86.74–97.85%**, wider/asymmetric relative to the point estimate. At 193/203 it is approximately **91.17–97.30%**. Recalculate a final protocol with Wilson/exact methods and, if required, assurance that the interval width or one-sided lower bound meets a prespecified target. Estimating sensitivity near 95% to approximate ±3 points is different from establishing sensitivity above 95%. These Wilson numbers are independently calculated here using the score formula discussed by FDA. [FDA interval methods](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/statistical-guidance-reporting-results-studies-evaluating-diagnostic-tests-guidance-industry-and-fda).

**Illustrative prevalence and enrichment scenarios:** all prevalences below are assumed recruitment-frame examples to be measured in pilots, not epidemiological estimates of epilepsy/PTSD or scan failure.

| Intended validation scenario | Positive/negative targets | Assumed natural target prevalence | Consecutive analyzable people needed; expected composition | With assumed 15% loss |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|
| Scan-level quality alert, Se .90±.05; Sp .95±.03 | ≥139 poor scans, ≥203 acceptable scans, one scan/person | 20% poor | max(695,254) = **695**: expected 139 poor, 556 acceptable | **818** recruited |
| Same alert in lower-risk workflow | Same | 10% poor | max(1,390,226) = **1,390**: expected 139 poor, 1,251 acceptable | **1,636** |
| One prespecified target-positive clinical state, Se/Sp .95±.03 | 203 positive, 203 negative | 30% positive | max(677,290) = **677**: expected about 203 positive, 474 negative | **797** |
| Higher-risk localization decision, Se .95±.03; Sp .95±.02 | 203 positive, 457 negative | 20% target-positive | max(1,015,572) = **1,015**: expected 203 positive, 812 negative | **1,195** |
| Same finding in a rarer referral stratum | Same | 5% target-positive | max(4,060,482) = **4,060**: expected 203 positive, 3,857 negative | **4,777** |

These expected yields are not guarantees of accruing enough positives; a real recruitment plan should allow random variation in prevalence or recruit to prespecified class denominators. For enrichment, directly recruit **203 positive + 457 negative = 660 analyzable clinical patients** for the last sensitivity/specificity targets. At 15% loss, recruit approximately **239 positive + 538 negative = 777**, with class-specific loss monitored. This reduces the analyzed cohort, but source-pool screening may still be large. Recruit relevant difficult negative cases, not just healthy volunteers. Spectrum differences can change sensitivity/specificity; enriched PPV, NPV and calibration do not automatically transport to practice. [FDA population/reference recommendations](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/statistical-guidance-reporting-results-studies-evaluating-diagnostic-tests-guidance-industry-and-fda).

**Bayesian arithmetic for clinical burden, not an observed device result:** with Se .90 and Sp .95 at 10% prevalence, among 1,000 people expect 90 true positives, 10 misses, 45 false positives and 855 true negatives. PPV = 90/(90+45) = **66.7%**. At 1% prevalence, expect 9 true positives and 49.5 false positives, so PPV = **15.4%**. The same assumed sensitivity/specificity therefore produces very different alert burdens. A continuous monitor additionally needs false alarms per unit time, because counting thousands of easy negative windows is not the same as a useful alarm policy. [FDA predictive-value discussion](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/statistical-guidance-reporting-results-studies-evaluating-diagnostic-tests-guidance-industry-and-fda).

### Gaps

- Sensitivity/specificity precision does not size continuous amplitude agreement, localization-distance tails, calibration curves, or incremental clinical utility. Those require pilot distributions and a chosen clinically acceptable difference.
- The denominator for surgical specificity must be defined: non-involved locations, patients without the target finding, or decisions that should not trigger intervention are different estimands. Millions of negative voxels do not provide millions of independent clinical negatives.
- Loss inflation is a recruitment allowance, not a license to delete failures. Describe all technical failures, abstentions and missing references; evaluate the consequences in the full enrolled population and prespecify sensitivity analyses.

## 4. How do clustering, site shift and rare harms change those numbers?

### Takeaway

Independent patient counts and independent site coverage are separate resources. More windows in the same patient cannot establish transport to a new patient; more patients at one familiar site cannot establish transport to a new scanner/site. TGA specifically identifies patient, site and acquisition dependencies when considering train/test independence. [TGA AI evidence requirements](https://www.tga.gov.au/products/medical-devices/software-and-artificial-intelligence-ai/manufacturing/artificial-intelligence-ai-and-medical-device-software-regulation/evidence-requirements-software-using-ai).

### Cited Findings

- Standard cluster-design methodology uses the illustrative equal-cluster design effect `DE = 1 + (m − 1) × ICC`. Its assumptions and analysis matter; it is not a general correction for distribution shift or a specific medical-device sample-size requirement. [Kerry and Bland, original statistics note](https://www.bmj.com/content/bmj/316/7130/549.full.pdf); [methods derivation and limitations](https://academic.oup.com/ije/article/44/3/1051/632956).
- TGA requires a statistically informed dataset-size justification, relevant Australian populations/subpopulations, independent testing and risk-proportionate external validation, alongside clinical and human-AI-team performance evaluation. It does not specify that a certain number of sites makes any model safe. [TGA AI evidence](https://www.tga.gov.au/products/medical-devices/software-and-artificial-intelligence-ai/manufacturing/artificial-intelligence-ai-and-medical-device-software-regulation/evidence-requirements-software-using-ai).

### Inferences

**Event clustering example — planning assumptions:** suppose the endpoint is sensitivity for discrete sustained arousal-loss episodes, with a mean of 10 reference-positive episodes per event-contributing person and within-person ICC .20. Then `DE = 1 + 9×.20 = 2.8`. To approximate the information in 139 independent positive events requires `ceil(139×2.8) = 390` observed positive episodes, nominally 39 event-contributing people. For 385 independent positives, `ceil(385×2.8) = 1,078` episodes, or at least 108 such people at that assumed yield. These are effective-event calculations, **not evidence that 39 or 108 people adequately represent the intended patient population**. Unequal event yield, severe autocorrelation, variable episode definitions and subject heterogeneity can invalidate the shortcut. Specificity/false-alarm burden needs its own denominator and clustering model. [Cluster-method basis](https://academic.oup.com/ije/article/44/3/1051/632956).

**Site clustering example — planning assumptions:** consider only reference-positive patients when sizing sensitivity. If 500 positives are sampled as 100/site at five sites, assumed within-site ICC .01 gives `DE = 1 + 99×.01 = 1.99`, effective n ≈ `500/1.99 = 251`. Spread across ten sites with 50/site, `DE = 1.49`, effective n ≈ 336. Calculate the negative-class problem separately; using total patients/site for every class would be wrong. This is an illustrative exchangeable-error model, not proof that site ICC is .01, nor that device errors actually follow this model. [Cluster-method basis](https://academic.oup.com/ije/article/44/3/1051/632956).

**Possible inflation for the enriched 660-person example:** if eight sites and class-specific ICC .01 are assumed, solving `n/[1 + (n/8 − 1)×.01] ≥ n_independent` gives:

```text
Positive class: n ≥ 203×.99 / [1 − 203×.01/8] = 269.31 -> 270
Negative class: n ≥ 457×.99 / [1 − 457×.01/8] = 1,055.23 -> 1,056
Total: 1,326 analyzable, before losses or finite-site corrections
```

This deliberately shows how fragile an apparently precise universal count would be. It is **not the recommended definitive trial size**: class sizes/sites are rounded, ICC is invented for illustration, only eight clusters provide limited information about site variability, and site shift can be systematic bias rather than exchangeable correlation. A final design needs multilevel simulation with estimated patient/site components and class allocation. [Methodological basis](https://academic.oup.com/ije/article/44/3/1051/632956).

**Design recommendations, proportionate to the locked indication:** freeze preprocessing, model, thresholds, quality gate and abstention rules before external testing; keep people and repeat scans together; document pretraining and public-dataset overlap; test future acquisition periods and genuinely new sites/hardware. If a new site supplies calibration labels, report an adapted model separately from untouched transfer. Analyze patient uncertainty and site heterogeneity using an appropriate hierarchy; include prediction uncertainty for a future site where supportable. Small site counts make site-level uncertainty unstable. None of these steps guarantees performance under unrepresented shifts. [FDA/IMDRF GMLP](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices/good-machine-learning-practice-medical-device-development-guiding-principles).

**Rare harm arithmetic — own calculation:** if zero independent patient-level harmful decisions occur, the exact one-sided 95% upper risk bound is `1 − .05^(1/n)`, approximately `3/n`. With n=300, the bound is approximately .994%; with n=3,000, .0998%. These calculations require independent representative exposures and do not establish absence of harm, safety in unrepresented abnormalities, or a regulatory acceptance rate. A few hundred clean cases cannot exclude rare serious harm. Patient-specific hallucination/localization failures need targeted challenge sets as well as representative cohorts. [Inference motivated by clinical validation and uncertainty requirements](https://www.imdrf.org/sites/default/files/docs/imdrf/final/technical/imdrf-tech-170921-samd-n41-clinical-evaluation_1.pdf).

### Gaps

- Actual within-person and within-site error ICCs, numbers of distinct events, prevalence, non-evaluable fraction and subgroup frequencies remain unknown for the proposed device. No fixed inflation factor is defensible yet.
- Repeated events can improve precision of an event estimand while contributing little new information about between-patient generalization. The effective-event calculation and person-level diversity requirements should both be reported.
- No number of training sites guarantees future-site validity. Validate the particular Australian workflows, populations, caps and scanners in the eventual claim, with continuing drift monitoring.

### Cited Findings

- Riley et al.’s development methodology ties sample size to outcome distribution, predictor complexity and anticipated fit, rather than a universal events-per-variable rule. **Clinical-utility arithmetic behind the table — own calculations:**

- **Monitoring:** for two independent proportions p1=.20 and p2=.10, two-sided alpha .05 and power .80, equal-arm normal approximation gives `n/arm = [1.96×sqrt(2×.15×.85) + .84×sqrt(.20×.80 + .10×.90)]² / .10² = 198.74`, rounded to 199; budget 200/arm. With 400 analyzable, `ceil(400/.85)=471`; budgeting balanced arms gives **236/arm =472 recruited**. These assumptions are illustrative, not an observed benefit. If scans/technologists/sites are randomized in clusters, this calculation is inadequate without the corresponding variance/cluster design. [Cluster-design methodological basis](https://academic.oup.com/ije/article/44/3/1051/632956).
- **Neurofeedback:** for independent continuous outcomes and equal arms, normal approximation `n/arm = 2×(1.96+.84)²/d²`. At standardized effect d=.5, n=62.72→63/arm, 126 total; at d=.3, n=174.22→175/arm, 350 total. Divide by .85 for attrition: about 149 and 412 recruited, or practical budgets 150 and 420. Pilot variance, clinically important symptom change, baseline adjustment, active comparator and follow-up will determine the actual study. These are not evidence that Amyg-EFP has either effect size. [Clinical endpoint precedent](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychres.2023.115711).
- **Why no invented presurgical noninferiority count:** the needed paired sample depends on discordant errors and the acceptable excess rate of wrong-sided localization/new deficits or loss of seizure benefit. A sensitivity precision study cannot supply that rate. Large independent datasets do not themselves justify the margin or the replacement claim. [Clinical endpoint context](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5272968/); [IMDRF intended-purpose clinical validation](https://www.imdrf.org/sites/default/files/docs/imdrf/final/technical/imdrf-tech-170921-samd-n41-clinical-evaluation_1.pdf).

**Training-data uncertainty:** the development ranges above are budget envelopes. Acquire staged increments of genuinely new people/sites; evaluate learning curves on independent development evaluation sets with uncertainty, including regional residuals and clinical strata. Reserve the final external test from all model/threshold selection. Compare simple spectral/ridge models and direct endpoint models before increasing capacity. More minutes from existing people can improve within-person estimation but cannot replace new pathology, person or site diversity. A small learning curve can flatten because of optimization, target noise, cohort restriction or sample variation; it does not identify a fundamental ceiling. General model-sample methodology supports outcome/model-specific planning but does not determine how many paired EEG-fMRI people a large generative model needs. [Riley development methods](https://www.bmj.com/content/368/bmj.m441).

### Gaps

- Budgets lack measured effect sizes, localization-error distributions, training scaling laws, expected loss/abstention rates and subgroup/site variance. Their role is to make decisions and costs concrete, not predict success.
- If a clinically important subgroup occurs in only 5% of the clinical stream, accruing 203 positive patients of that subgroup may require approximately 4,060 patients before losses; simply allocating 1,000 total development subjects does not solve rare-abnormality validation.
- Broad functional substitution may remain infeasible even with many thousands of paired people if the individual’s relevant information is absent or unstable in EEG. Conversely, a narrow alert or intervention may be useful without high-fidelity regional synthesis. Neither possibility is established by the current internal data.

## 7. How should hallucinated or erased abnormalities be managed, and what must the writer not overclaim?

### Takeaway

Plausible-looking synthetic functional images may convey confidence without preserving a patient’s abnormal function. The clinically defensible near-term target is a labeled advisory or narrowly validated surrogate/intervention, with a defined human action, uncertainty and escalation policy; functional replacement needs direct evidence for the locked decision. Imaging-method research documents reconstruction instabilities and missed small structural changes, but does not establish the rate of those failures in this EEG-to-fMRI project. [Antun et al., peer-reviewed PNAS study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7720232/).

### Cited Findings

- Antun et al., 2020, show that studied learned reconstruction systems can produce large artifacts after small perturbations and fail to preserve small changes. This evidence concerns image reconstruction systems with different inputs; applying the risk to EEG-derived functional maps is a reasoned safety concern, not an observed project-specific harm rate. [Original study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7720232/).
- Bhadra et al., 2021, develop a framework for hallucinations in tomographic reconstruction and the influence of learned priors. It supports separating prior-driven plausibility from measurement-supported patient detail. [Peer-reviewed original paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8673588/).
- TGA explicitly includes overreliance and foreseeable misuse in human-factors considerations, together with transparent labeling of inputs/limitations and lifecycle risk management. [TGA AI evidence guidance](https://www.tga.gov.au/products/medical-devices/software-and-artificial-intelligence-ai/manufacturing/artificial-intelligence-ai-and-medical-device-software-regulation/evidence-requirements-software-using-ai).

### Inferences

- **Failure mechanisms to test, not established findings:** learned population priors could normalize away unusual activation, impose a typical hemisphere/region, reproduce a global physiological fluctuation across many regions, or create a convincing but incorrect focal pattern. Altered anatomy, lesions, disease-related neurovascular coupling and absent scalp visibility are particularly relevant challenge conditions. These concerns follow from the reconstruction evidence and indication-specific clinical validation principle. [Antun et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7720232/); [IMDRF](https://www.imdrf.org/sites/default/files/docs/imdrf/final/technical/imdrf-tech-170921-samd-n41-clinical-evaluation_1.pdf).
- At point of use, prefer “wakefulness advisory,” “estimated target signal,” or a specified validated decision output. When images are shown, clearly label them synthetic functional estimates, preserve provenance, and show quality/coverage limitations. Avoid presenting inferred anatomy or a normal-looking image as evidence that pathology is absent. A trained clinician reviewing a convincing incorrect image is not itself validation. Test how the human-AI team actually behaves. [TGA human-factors/transparency guidance](https://www.tga.gov.au/products/medical-devices/software-and-artificial-intelligence-ai/manufacturing/artificial-intelligence-ai-and-medical-device-software-regulation/evidence-requirements-software-using-ai).
- A safety evaluation should count clinically important additions and omissions, wrong-sided decisions, failed/abstained cases, and downstream actions with independent adjudication. Include abnormality-enriched challenge sets and representative prospective cases. Calibration/uncertainty methods require validation under shift; an ensemble’s small spread is not a guarantee that its common prior is correct. [Inference from learned-prior analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8673588/); [GMLP](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices/good-machine-learning-practice-medical-device-development-guiding-principles).

**Writer guardrails:**

1. Say “no validated diagnostic/presurgical EEG-only fMRI replacement was identified in this search,” not “no EEG-fMRI clinical device or trial exists.” Prism and narrow clinical neurofeedback studies exist; their indication is different.
2. Keep functional-MRI substitution distinct from anatomical MRI. EEG cannot supply individual anatomical MRI; preserve standard anatomical imaging and any clinically required reference tests.
3. Label every proposed participant/site range, prevalence, ICC, performance target and utility effect as a planning assumption. No sample count guarantees learning, regulatory success or clinical accuracy.
4. State independent patient/event denominators. Do not count 29 scans as 29 people, 106 surgeries as 106 people, or thousands of windows/voxels as independent subjects.
5. Do not equate temporal R, spatial R, static-FC similarity, SSIM, classifier accuracy and clinical sensitivity/specificity. Give definitions/splits or explicitly say they were unavailable.
6. Do not infer replacement validity from EEG-informed analysis of actual MRI, target engagement, healthy-subject reconstruction or unpaired disease classification.
7. Do not describe internal replay as prospective live scanner validation, .227 versus .228 as equivalence, global-component sensitivity as proof of either artifact or regional specificity, or the small learning curve as a fundamental ceiling.
8. Distinguish peer-reviewed research, conference/workshop reports, preprints, abstracts and inaccessible methods. Novel 2026 full-brain methods exist; their existence does not establish clinical validation, and inaccessible methods must not be filled in from AI summaries.
9. Precision is not minimum acceptable accuracy: a 95% point estimate with a broad lower bound does not establish sensitivity ≥95%. Narrow subgroup/site intervals and clinically meaningful comparative utility may dominate overall sample needs.
10. Phrase oversight concretely: who reviews which output, when to abstain, what happens after an alert, and what standard test is used when uncertain. “Human in the loop” alone is not evidence of safe care.

### Gaps

- No prospective clinical utility, pathological-abnormality preservation or presurgical safety evidence was supplied for the internal NeuroBOLT/ridge/arousal-monitor prototypes. Their current clinical claims should remain proportionate to research feasibility.
- The definitive datasets cannot be finalized without a locked indication, clinical error tolerances, reference/adjudication protocol, pilot variance/ICC/prevalence and a prospective decision policy. These are the concrete next inputs to obtain, rather than choosing an unsupported universal number of people.
